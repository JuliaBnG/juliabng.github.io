Optimum Contribution Selection

Optimum contribution selection (OCS) chooses the genetic contributions $\mathbf{c}$ (or $\mathbf{w}$) of selection candidates to the next generation so that expected genetic merit is maximised while the group coancestry, and hence the rate of inbreeding, is controlled. Breeding.jl provides two formulations:

  • the constraint form of Meuwissen (1997): maximise $\mathbf{c}'\mathbf{u}$ subject to an upper bound on $\mathbf{c}'\mathbf{A}\mathbf{c}$ (TM1997, OC_fixsex2);
  • the penalty form of Fogg et al. (2026), which also penalises the uncertainty of the EBVs (rocs, alias robust_ocs).

The input EBVs, prediction-error (co)variances and relationship matrix come directly from the rest of the package (e.g. solutions, lhs_inverse, pev) and from RelationshipMatrices.jl (nrm, a genomic $\mathbf{G}$, …).

Meuwissen (1997): constraint on average relationship

\[\max_{\mathbf{c}} \ \mathbf{c}'\mathbf{u} \quad \text{s.t.} \quad \mathbf{c}'\mathbf{A}\mathbf{c} \le K, \quad \mathbf{Q}'\mathbf{c} = \left[\tfrac12, \tfrac12\right]', \quad \mathbf{c} \ge \mathbf{0}\]

where $\mathbf{Q}$ holds male and female indicators. With a target inbreeding $k_t = k_{t-1} + \Delta F (1 - k_{t-1})$, $K = 2k_t$. The Lagrangian solution is iterated, dropping candidates with negative contributions, until all remaining contributions are positive. If $K$ is below the minimum attainable $\mathbf{c}'\mathbf{A}\mathbf{c}$, a warning is issued and the minimum-coancestry contributions are returned.

c = TM1997(DataFrame(tmi = ebv, sex = sex), A, K)   # sex: 1 male, 0 female
c = TM1997(ebv, sex, A, K)                          # equivalent vector method

OC_fixsex2 solves the male side when the female contributions are fixed (e.g. already-selected dams), with A ordered males first, then the fixed females.

Fogg et al. (2026): robust OCS

\[\max_{\mathbf{l} \le \mathbf{w} \le \mathbf{u}} \ \mathbf{w}'\bar{\boldsymbol{\mu}} - \kappa \sqrt{\mathbf{w}'\boldsymbol{\Omega}\mathbf{w}} - \frac{\lambda}{2} \mathbf{w}'\boldsymbol{\Sigma}\mathbf{w} \quad \text{s.t.} \quad \mathbf{M}\mathbf{w} = \mathbf{m}\]

\[\bar{\boldsymbol{\mu}}\]

are the EBVs, $\boldsymbol{\Omega}$ their prediction-error covariance matrix (or only its diagonal, the PEVs) and $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}$ the relationship matrix. The two penalty weights give four regimes:

λκRegime
00truncation selection (TS)
0> 0robust truncation selection (RTS)
> 00optimum contribution selection (OCS)
> 0> 0robust OCS (ROCS)

TS is solved exactly by sorting; OCS is a single convex quadratic programme solved by an active-set method; RTS and ROCS iterate convex quadratic subproblems whose fixed point satisfies the KKT conditions of the robust problem. sex enforces $\sum w_i = \tfrac12$ within each sex (otherwise $\sum w_i = 1$), and lb/ub bound individual contributions.

The scale of λ depends on whether $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}$ is a relationship ($\mathbf{A}$, $\mathbf{G}$) or a coancestry ($\mathbf{A}/2$) matrix; λ and κ are trade-off weights, not targets, and are usually swept to trace a Pareto frontier.

# Mrode Example 4.1: EBVs and their prediction-error covariance
m    = mme(y, X, [RandomEffect(Z, Ai, 20.0)]; σ²e = 40.0)
_, (u,) = solutions(m, solve_mme(m))
Ω    = Matrix(lhs_inverse(m)[3:10, 3:10]) .* 40.0
A    = nrm(ped)

r = rocs(vec(u), A; Ω = Ω, sex = sex, λ = 2.0, κ = 1.0, ub = 0.25)
r.w, r.merit, r.std_error, r.coancestry, r.converged
w, objective, merit, coancestry, std_error = r      # destructuring
