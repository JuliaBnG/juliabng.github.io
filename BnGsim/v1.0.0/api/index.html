API Reference

What 1.0 promises

From 1.0 on, these do not change incompatibly within a major version:

  • the names exported by BnGsim (below), with their documented behaviour;
  • the command line: interface 1 and its exit codes ("Command line");
  • the scenario schema, version 1, under the policy of "Scenarios";
  • the output files, outputs_version 1 ("Output files");
  • the reproducibility invariants: results independent of the number of threads and of stopping and resuming, and results_sha256.

Outside the promise: names that are documented here but not exported (reach them as BnGsim.name; for example BnGsim.philox4x64, BnGsim.keyed_int and BnGsim.transport, unexported in 0.38), the BnGsim.Legacy module (its crossover sampler is BnGsim.Legacy.crossovers, separate from the exported crossovers), the coefficients of resource_estimate (recalibrated as the code changes), and numerical results of a scenario across BnGsim versions (each run records its versions and hashes).

Types of BnGStructs.jl appear in this API (Haplotype, VariantMap, LocusSet, for example in founder_haplotypes and genomes). They are promised as far as BnGsim's compat bound on BnGStructs allows: a breaking release of BnGStructs can change them, and BnGsim then states the change in its release notes.

BnGsim.MeiosisMap — Type
MeiosisMap(species, map::VariantMap)

MeiosisMap(species, map::VariantMap; male = nothing, female = nothing, interference = 1.0)

The genome layout used by make_progeny!, built once: for every chromosome its physical start and end (genome-wide bp), its genetic length in Morgans (from BnGStructs.map_length), and the range of loci of map on it. The map is checked against the species with BnGStructs.check_coordinates.

Sex-specific maps: male and female are the genetic lengths of the chromosomes in the meioses of sires and dams (default: those of the species); a sire's gamete uses the male map, a dam's the female.

Crossover interference (interference = ν): the chiasmata of the four-strand bundle form a stationary gamma renewal process along the chromosome, two per Morgan, with gaps $\mathrm{Gamma}(\nu, 1/(2\nu))$ (the gamma model; McPeek and Speed 1995, Broman and Weber 2000), and each chiasma is in the gamete with probability ½ (no chromatid interference). The first chiasma lies at $UX$, $X$ the length-biased gap covering the start of the chromosome, $\mathrm{Gamma}(\nu + 1, 1/(2\nu))$, and $U$ uniform, so the process is stationary. A gamete then has on average $L$ crossovers on a chromosome of $L$ Morgans. ν = 1 is Haldane's map (no interference), drawn as a Poisson count as before; ν > 1 is positive interference (human data fit ν ≈ 4.3). Positions are linear in the physical length.

BnGsim.MeiosisRecord — Type
MeiosisRecord

Crossovers of the gametes made by one call of make_progeny!. Gamete g is column g of the offspring Haplotype (2i-1 from the sire, 2i from the dam of mating i).

  • parent[g]: the parent (column of the mating plan) that made it;
  • phase[c, g]: true if chromosome c starts on the parent's second haplotype;
  • crossovers(rec, g): genome-wide positions of its crossovers, in increasing order within each chromosome.

With the parental records this determines the founder origin of every segment, the input of segment-level IBD.

BnGsim.PedigreeView — Type
PedigreeView(sire, dam, ids)

A pedigree restricted to the animals ids, in the form RelationshipMatrices expects: dense rows 1:length(ids), parents before offspring, and 0 for an unknown parent. Row r is the animal v.ids[r]; its parents are v.sire[r] and v.dam[r], as rows, 0 when the parent is unknown or not in the view.

sire and dam are the full pedigree by id (0 unknown), with every parent's id below its offspring's, as in a Population; ids need not be sorted or unique and are stored in ascending order, which is then a topological order. The view is the boundary between the domain ids, which are never reused, and the row indices of the relationship matrices and mixed-model equations built on it: a matrix built from v.sire and v.dam is indexed by rows, and row_to_id and id_to_row translate.

See also pedigree_view.

BnGsim.Population — Type
Population

Animals of a breeding program, one row per animal in order of birth. Animal ids are 1:n and equal the row; nothing is ever removed, so the equality holds (invariant I4 holds trivially). Tables that leave the population carry the id explicitly (pedigree), and relationship matrices on a subset are indexed through a PedigreeView.

Read it through these functions; its fields are internal and may change in any release:

  • pedigree: ids, parents, sex, birth cohort, exit, herd, and per trait the true value, phenotype and EBV, as a table;
  • tbv, ebv, phenotypes: the same values as n × k matrices; records: every record, with its time step;
  • traits, trait_qtl, covariances: the traits, their QTL, and the covariance matrices of the next evaluation;
  • genomes, retained: the haplotypes kept;
  • alive, observed, recorded, parity, meioses, current_step, ngenerations, generation_ids, length.

Build one with founder_population, extend it with step!, and save or restore it with save_checkpoint and load_checkpoint.

BnGsim.RandomSign — Type
RandomSign(d)

Effects drawn from the distribution d with a random sign: x from d, then -x with probability ½ (one draw from d, then one Bool, per effect). With a Gamma d, the effect distribution of Meuwissen, Hayes and Goddard (2001): symmetric, with the heavy tails of the Gamma. Used as a Trait's da (scenario family "gamma" with "sign": "random"). Only rand, mean (0) and var ($E[x^2]$ of d) are defined.

BnGsim.Scenario — Type
Scenario

A validated simulation scenario, read from JSON with read_scenario or parse_scenario, written with write_scenario, and run with run_scenario. Every optional value is filled with its default, so the canonical JSON (scenario_json) states the whole simulation. Two scenarios are equal when all their values are.

The JSON document (schema "bngsim-scenario", version 1) has these keys; those without a default are required.

Top level

  • schema: "bngsim-scenario"; schema_version: 1;
  • name: a label;
  • seed: the master seed, an integer in 0:2^64-1 (every random stream derives from it through SimKey);
  • replicates (1): replicates 1:replicates;
  • steps: time steps of the breeding scheme.

species either {"preset": "Cattle"} (one of Cattle, Sheep, Goat, Pig, Horse, Dog, Cat, Chicken, Rabbit), or {"name", "chromosome"} with autosome lengths in bp; and either M, bp per Morgan (1e8), or morgan, the genetic length of each chromosome. The canonical form lists name, chromosome and morgan. Also morgan_female (null): the genetic lengths in the meioses of dams, with morgan then those of sires (the historical population, simulated by FisherWright with one map, uses their average); and interference (1.0): the shape ν of the gamma model of crossover interference (1: none, Haldane; see MeiosisMap), in the breeding steps.

founders: the historical population, run with FisherWright from no variation: ne diploids for nt generations at mr mutations per 1e8 bp per meiosis (1.0); then decline ([]), the census sizes of further generations (e.g. a decline to a breed); and nid, the founders sampled from the last generation. With shared_history (true) histories (1) historical populations serve the replicates, replicate r using number ((r - 1) mod histories) + 1, each replicate sampling its own founders; otherwise each replicate runs its own. history_dir (null): read those histories from a directory written by bngsim history (write_histories), whose historical parameters must equal the scenario's (the path is relative to the working directory). herds (1): the number of herds the founders are spread over at random; offspring are born in their dam's herd (contemporary groups, see c2 of the traits). popsize (null): the census trajectory of the nt historical generations, {"bottleneck": {"size", "start", "duration", "recovery" (ne)}} or {"growth": {"rate", "start" (0), "limit" (null)}} (FisherWright's bottleneck and growth from ne); sexratio (null): a fixed proportion of males among the parents of every historical generation (null: Bernoulli(½) sexes). selection (null): a quantitative trait under selection in the history, {"nsites", "da", "mu", "sigma_e" (0), "fitness"}: nsites loci drawn over the genome (keyed), effects of the derived allele from da (as a trait's), derived alleles by mutation at rate mu per site per haplotype per generation, phenotype = genotypic value plus N(0, sigma_e²), and fitness {"model": "stabilizing", "theta" (0), "omega2"} or {"model": "directional", "beta"} (FisherWright's TraitFitness, additive loci). With loci.qtl.from = "history" its loci are the QTL.

loci: qtl, {"nlc", "maf", "from"} (maf 0.0: loci with minor allele frequency above maf among the founders; from "neutral": nlc loci sampled from the variants of the history, or "history": the loci of founders.selection segregating among the founders above maf, with their historical effects times one factor (scale_qtl), one trait only; with from = "history", nlc (null: all) draws that many of those loci at random, and rho (1.0, in [-1, 1]) correlates the breeding effects with the historical ones: rho z_a + √(1 - rho²) z, z new effects from the trait's da, both standardised); chip (null), the same for the genotyping panel, needed by :gblup and :ssgblup; overlap ("disjoint"): whether chip loci may also be QTL ("allowed").

traits: a non-empty array of {"name", "h2" (0.25), "sigma_a" (1.0), "mu" (0.0), "sex" (2), "age" (0), "da"}, as BnGStructs.Trait: sex 0 females, 1 males, 2 both; age the whole time step of the record; da the distribution of the QTL effects before scaling, {"family": "normal", "mean": 0.0, "sd": 1.0} (default), {"family": "gamma", "shape", "scale": 1.0, "sign": "positive"} ("sign": "random" for effects of either sign, RandomSign), {"family": "laplace", "scale": 1.0} (location 0) or {"family": "spike_slab", "pi", "sd": 1.0, "spike_sd": 0.0} (SpikeSlab: a fraction pi of the effects from Normal(0, sd), the others 0, or from Normal(0, spike_sd)). Effects are scaled to sigma_a afterwards, so only the shape of the distribution matters; with several traits, only the first keeps it (see eqtl). c2 (0.0, in [0, 1)): the variance of the contemporary-group effects (herd × time step of the record) as a fraction of the founders' sigma_a² + sigma_e²; h2 is then the heritability within groups. Any c2 > 0 makes evaluations fit the groups as fixed effects. records (1): with m > 1, an animal gets a record at every time step it is alive from the trait's age on, at most m, with permanent environment from repeatability (null: h2, none; in [h2, 1)). Traits with repeated and single records can be mixed; see pe_correlation. missing (0.0): the probability that a record is missing at random; unrecorded_herds (0.0): the probability that a herd never records the trait (both in [0, 1), keyed draws that leave the other records unchanged).

genetic_correlation, residual_correlation, pe_correlation (identity): k × k correlation matrices of k traits, as arrays of rows. The residual correlation applies among the records of one animal made at the same time step; pe_correlation among the permanent-environment effects of the traits with repeated records (repeatability > h2; its other entries are not used).

scheme: the keywords of Scheme, in ASCII: noff, nsire, ndam, evaluation, selection, K, lambda, kappa, ub, blend, max_sire_age, max_dam_age, reml, weights, survival, cull_unselected, genotyped_cohorts, ocs, delta_F, record_meiosis, window, ancestor_generations, retain, report_reliability, mating, pair_limit, min_sire_age, min_dam_age, max_sire_offspring, max_dam_offspring, max_parity, with the same defaults; null for K and delta_F (unset) and for ancestor_generations (all). The chip loci come from loci.chip.

Unknown keys, duplicate keys and values of the wrong type are errors.

BnGsim.ScenarioSize — Type
ScenarioSize

The quantities of a scenario that its memory and time grow with, computed before it runs:

  • alleles: the expected number of derived alleles a haplotype of the historical population carries, $μL \min(t, 4N)$ for N = ne over t generations (nt plus the decline): $μL$ new ones per generation, until fixation balances them at the neutral equilibrium $4NμL$;
  • loci: the expected number of loci segregating among the 2nid founder haplotypes, $4NμL \ln(1 + 2n_{id} t / 4N)$, the total branch length of their genealogy truncated at the start of the run (the population starts without variation);
  • animals: founders and offspring of the run, nid + steps × noff;
  • evaluated: the largest pedigree evaluated, nid + (steps - 1) × noff;
  • genotyped: the animals in the dense relationship matrices of :gblup (all evaluated) and :ssgblup (the last genotyped_cohorts cohorts, or all); 0 otherwise;
  • cohort: the largest cohort (the per-step summary forms its dense relationship matrix);
  • histories: the historical populations held in memory at once (K of founders.histories when shared; 1 otherwise), and census, the largest census of the history (FisherWright sizes its buffers by it);
  • records: the repeated records made over the run (record mode: about one per animal, trait and step of its recorded life);
  • equations: the mixed-model equations of the evaluations (animal and, with repeated records, permanent-environment effects).

With a census trajectory (founders.popsize), N in alleles and loci is the harmonic mean of the census over the historical generations.

BnGsim.Scheme — Type
Scheme(; noff, nsire, ndam, evaluation = :pblup, selection = :truncation,
       K = NaN, λ = 0.0, κ = 0.0, ub = 1.0, chip = Int32[], blend = 0.05,
       max_sire_age = 0, max_dam_age = 0, reml = false, weights = Float64[],
       survival = Float64[], cull_unselected = false, genotyped_cohorts = 0,
       ocs = :fixed, ΔF = NaN, record_meiosis = false, report_reliability = false,
       mating = :random, pair_limit = 0, min_sire_age = 0, min_dam_age = 0,
       max_sire_offspring = 0, max_dam_offspring = 0, max_parity = 0)

A selection and mating scheme, applied every time step:

  • noff: offspring per step (one per mating);
  • nsire, ndam: parents selected by truncation, and the number of each sex used by random selection;
  • evaluation: :tbv (true values, an oracle), :phenotype (own records), :pblup (animal model with the pedigree relationship matrix), :gblup (animal model with $G^* = (1-w)G + wA$, w = blend, G VanRaden at the loci chip with base allele frequencies from the founders), :ssgblup (single-step, $H^{-1}$ of Legarra et al. 2009 and Christensen & Lund 2010 from RelationshipMatrices.hinv, with the animals of the last genotyped_cohorts cohorts genotyped at the loci chip (0: all animals), $G$ as for :gblup and blended with $A_{22}$ by blend; with every animal genotyped it equals :gblup). With several traits the animal model is multi-trait, with an intercept per trait and residuals correlated within animals;
  • weights: the index weights $w$ (one per trait; empty: all 1). Candidates are ranked on $I = w'\hat a$, which with economic values as weights and multi-trait BLUP EBVs is the optimal index for the aggregate genotype $H = w'g$ (Henderson 1963). With :phenotype it is $w'y$, a fixed-weight index, not the optimal phenotypic index;
  • selection: :truncation (best nsire males and ndam females on the index, equal numbers of offspring), :ocs (optimum contributions; see ocs below), :rocs (Breeding.rocs with λ, κ, the prediction error covariance of the candidates' index as Ω, and an upper bound ub on each parent's contribution, in rocs units where each sex contributes ½; ub = 0.5/nsire with λ = κ = 0 is truncation selection), or :random (nsire and ndam chosen at random, a neutral reference);
  • max_sire_age, max_dam_age: the oldest age, in time steps, at which an animal can be selected as a parent (age 0: born in the current step). 0 for both gives discrete generations; larger values give overlapping generations, with all eligible ages competing on one criterion. With :ocs and :rocs the contributions are those of the candidates to the next cohort, without the age-class weighting of Meuwissen & Sonesson (1998) or Bijma & Woolliams (2000); the constraint then does not bound the long-term rate of inbreeding exactly;
  • reml: if true, the covariance components of :pblup and :gblup are re-estimated by AI-REML (Breeding.jl) from all records before every evaluation, starting from the previous estimates, once every trait has a record on an animal with a known parent (before that, as with the founders' records alone, genetic and residual components are not separable and the true values are used); otherwise the true values of the founders are used. With all records and the full pedigree, REML estimates the base-population components even under selection (Sorensen & Kennedy 1984; infinitesimal model). Several traits are estimated with AI steps on the Cholesky factors (param = :cholesky of Breeding.reml), which keeps the estimates positive definite; early estimates may lie on the boundary (e.g. a genetic correlation near ±1 after one cohort);
  • ocs: the constraint of :ocs. :fixed: Breeding.TM1997 with $c'Ac \le K$ at every step (average coancestry $K/2$). The others hold the rate of inbreeding at the target ΔF per generation (see ocs_contributions): :cohort constrains the mean kinship of the newborn cohort; :age_year the mean kinship of the population at the next step, age classes weighted by their remaining expected contributions (Meuwissen & Sonesson 1998; optiSel), with the per-step increase set by the estimated generation interval; :age_generation the same with the generation interval of the contributions being chosen (a per-generation constraint, Sonesson et al. 2000). With discrete generations the three coincide;
  • mating: how the slots of the selected sires and dams (one per offspring) are paired, see mating_plan: :random, :min_coancestry (the pairing of least total coancestry), :assortative or :disassortative (by the index);
  • min_sire_age, min_dam_age: the youngest age at which an animal can be a parent (time steps; 0: from birth), at most the maximum ages; the founders, the adult base population, are parents from step 0;
  • max_sire_offspring, max_dam_offspring: at most this many offspring per parent and time step (0: no limit), e.g. a litter size, or natural service against AI. Offspring numbers are capped and the rest spread over the other parents in proportion to their contributions; an ArgumentError when the selected parents cannot have noff offspring under the limits;
  • max_parity: at most this many time steps with offspring per dam (0: no limit; parity counts them);
  • pair_limit: at most this many offspring per sire–dam pair (0: no limit; 1: no full sibs, a factorial-type design), with :random or :min_coancestry;
  • record_meiosis: keep the crossover records of every cohort (meioses), for realized IBD from founder segments (founder_segments); about 8 bytes per crossover;
  • report_reliability: report the mean reliability of the candidates of each step from the prediction error variances of the evaluation (:pblup, :gblup, :ssgblup; see step!). It costs a solve of the mixed-model equations per candidate and trait, so it is off by default (the column is then NaN), and the resource estimate does not include it (on 5500 animals and 10 steps of PBLUP it took 0.3 s more than the 0.4 s of the run);
  • survival: survival[a + 1] is the probability that an animal alive at age a survives to age a + 1 (the last entry for all older ages; empty: no deaths). Deaths are drawn between steps (Domain.SURVIVAL), independently of genotype and records (missing at random), so they bias neither BLUP nor REML;
  • cull_unselected: if true, eligible animals of age 1 or more that are not selected as parents at a step are culled. Young candidates (age 0) are kept for the next step. Culling depends on the EBV, so the animals left are a selected sample; BLUP and REML with all records and the pedigree account for that;
  • window: if positive, :pblup, :gblup and :ssgblup use the records of the animals born in the last window time steps only (the current cohort and the window - 1 before it), on the pedigree of these animals and their ancestors traced for ancestor_generations generations (a PedigreeView); the other animals get no EBV (NaN). It must exceed the maximum ages, so that every candidate is evaluated. 0 (default): all records and the whole pedigree. Under selection, discarding the records of earlier cohorts and the parents beyond the traced depth biases BLUP (Henderson 1975), so a window trades accuracy for the size of the equations; REML with reml = true is then estimated from the window's records too;
  • ancestor_generations: generations of ancestors traced above the animals of the window (default: all; then the relationships among the window's animals are exact, and only records are discarded);
  • retain: :all (default) keeps every genome. :active drops, after each step, the genomes no later step can use: those of animals that can no longer be parents (dead, culled, or past the maximum age of their sex) and are not genotyped by :ssgblup (outside the last genotyped_cohorts cohorts). The founders are kept (the base allele frequencies). Pedigree and records are kept for every animal; memory then grows with the animals that are still candidates, not with all animals born. Not with :gblup or :ssgblup with every animal genotyped, which need every genome. A dropped genome cannot be read later (genomes throws), so summaries of past cohorts that need genomes are taken during the run.

Dead and culled animals are not candidates, and a record taken at an age the animal did not reach is missing.

BnGsim.SimKey — Type
SimKey(seed; replicate = 0, generation = 0, domain = 0, stage = 0, entity = 0, subcounter = 0)
SimKey(base::SimKey; field = value, ...)

The coordinate of one random stream:

FieldTypeMeaning
seedUInt64master seed of the scenario
replicateUInt32replicate number
generationUInt32generation or time step
domainUInt16Domain code: meiosis, phenotype, mating, …
stageUInt16a stable code of the event within the domain, never a position in a schedule
entityUInt32the animal, mating or locus the draws are for; 0 when population-scoped
subcounterUInt64a further index, e.g. sire (0) or dam (1) gamete

The second form copies base and replaces the named fields, so callers derive keys from a scenario key.

keyed_rng turns a key into a random number generator. Two keys that differ in any field give unrelated streams.

Layout

The Philox4x64-10 key is (seed, replicate | KEY_LAYOUT << 32) and the counter is (generation << 32 | domain << 16 | stage, entity, subcounter, 0). The four 64-bit outputs are the state of a Random.Xoshiro. This layout is fixed; the golden test in test/keys.jl guards it.

BnGsim.SpikeSlab — Type
SpikeSlab(π, sd; spike_sd = 0.0)

A spike-and-slab effect distribution: with probability π an effect from the slab Normal(0, sd), otherwise from the spike, exactly 0 (or Normal(0, spike_sd) with spike_sd > 0). One uniform, then one normal when the effect is not exactly zero, per effect. With a point-mass spike, about a fraction 1 - π of the QTL have no effect (scenario family "spike_slab"). Only rand, mean (0) and var are defined.

BnGsim._execute — Method
_execute(dir, s, reps; checkpoint_every = 0, should_stop = (r, g) -> false)

Run replicates reps of s with its state in dir/.bngsim-run, or continue a run whose state is there. After step g of replicate r, if should_stop(r, g) the state is saved and (:cancelled, r, g) returned; with checkpoint_every = k > 0 it is also saved every k steps, so that a killed process can be resumed from there. BnGsim.aggregate — Method
aggregate(summary::DataFrame) -> DataFrame

Aggregate the per-step summaries of several replicates (the table of run_scenario, with a column replicate). One row per time step (generation), with replicates, the number of replicates at that step, and for every other column c of the summary c_mean, c_sd (with the n - 1 divisor) and c_se (c_sd / √n) over the replicates, NaN values left out (n then counts the others). Also aggregated are the changes per step within each replicate: dG (of mean, the index of the traits) and dG_<trait> (of mean_<trait>) for the genetic means, dF, the rate of inbreeding $(F_t - F_{t-1})/(1 - F_{t-1})$, and dFhet, the same rate of the loss of heterozygosity Fhet; and the depletion of the genetic variance within each replicate, var_ratio and genic_ratio, the variance and the genic variance relative to the replicate's first step (with selection, genic_ratio falls with allele frequencies changing at the QTL, and var_ratio below it with the negative disequilibrium of the Bulmer effect).

With a column history (the historical population of each replicate) in which at least two histories have several replicates each, also histories, the number of histories at that step, and for every column c c_sdh, the standard deviation of the history means of c, and c_seh (c_sdh / √K over the K histories): with replicates nested in histories, c_seh is the standard error of the mean over histories, which includes the variation between histories that c_se leaves out (replicates of one history are not independent draws of the history).

The rows are sorted by replicate before summing, so the result is the same, to the bit, whatever the order of the replicates in summary.

BnGsim.cli — Method
cli(args::Vector{String}; stdout = stdout, stderr = stderr) -> Int

The bngsim command line: run the command in args and return its exit code (ExitCode). bin/bngsim calls it as exit(BnGsim.cli(ARGS)).

  • validate SCENARIO.json: read and check the scenario. Prints {"status": "valid", "scenario_sha256", ...} or {"status": "invalid", "errors": [...]} (exit code 3).
  • estimate SCENARIO.json: the expected peak memory, a conservative bound, the disk written and a rough run time (resource_estimate).
  • run SCENARIO.json --out DIR [--replicates A:B] [--memory-limit SIZE]: run the scenario (replicates A:B, all by default) and write to DIR, which must not exist or be empty: scenario.json (the canonical scenario), summary.csv (the per-step summaries of every replicate) and provenance.json. Prints {"status": "completed", "outputs": ...}. With --memory-limit, a scenario whose memory bound exceeds SIZE is refused before it runs (exit code 5). While it runs, the state lives in DIR/.bngsim-run/; a file DIR/CANCEL stops it after the current time step, with the state saved (exit code 7), and with --checkpoint-every K the state is also saved every K steps, so that a killed process can be resumed. With --archive none|chip|qtl|all, an archive of each replicate (write_archive) is written to DIR/archive/r<N>/ when it finishes, and listed in provenance.json.
  • merge DIR... --out OUT: combine completed runs of the same scenario (equal results_sha256) with disjoint replicates (e.g. split over hosts with --replicates) into OUT: scenario.json, summary.csv (rows ordered by replicate, the bytes of a single run of all of them), aggregate.csv and provenance.json listing the runs merged.
  • history SCENARIO.json --out DIR: simulate the founders.histories historical populations of the scenario once and store them in DIR (write_histories); a scenario with founders.history_dir = DIR and the same historical parameters then reads them instead of simulating. validate, run and resume refuse a history directory whose manifest does not fit the scenario (exit code 3 or 4).
  • resume DIR: continue a stopped or killed run from its last saved state (removing DIR/CANCEL). The outputs are those of an uninterrupted run, byte for byte; provenance.json lists every session. The state must come from the same BnGsim and Julia versions (exit code 4 otherwise).

BnGsim.eqtl — Method
eqtl(hap::Haplotype, loci, R, ts::AbstractVector{<:AbstractTrait};
     rng = Random.default_rng(), atol = 1e-8) -> MultiTraitQTL

Sample additive QTL effects of several traits at shared loci so that the genetic values of the individuals in hap have mean 0, standard deviations ts[i].σₐ and correlation matrix R, exactly in that sample.

Effects of trait i are drawn from ts[i].da, independently across traits. With $\mathbf{C}$ the sample covariance matrix of the resulting genetic values and $\boldsymbol\Sigma = \mathbf{D}\mathbf{R}\mathbf{D}$ ($\mathbf{D}$ the diagonal of the σₐ) the target, the effect matrix $\mathbf{A}$ is replaced by $\mathbf{A}\mathbf{L}_C^{-\top}\mathbf{L}_\Sigma^\top$ ($\mathbf{L}$ the Cholesky factors). The transform is upper triangular: the effects of trait 1 are only rescaled and keep the shape of their distribution; those of trait i > 1 are linear combinations of the draws for traits 1…i, so only their first two moments are controlled. Means go to the intercepts.

Needs at least as many QTL as traits and more individuals than traits. The realised covariance is checked and an ArgumentError is thrown if it misses Σ by more than atol (relative to the largest variance).

BnGsim.eqtl — Method
eqtl(hap::Haplotype, loci, t::AbstractTrait; rng = Random.default_rng(), atol = 1e-8) -> TraitQTL

Sample additive QTL effects of trait t at loci (a LocusSet or locus indices) and scale them so that the genetic values of the individuals in hap, the base population, have standard deviation t.σₐ and mean 0.

  • Effects are drawn from t.da and multiplied by one factor, so their distribution keeps its shape and sign (a Gamma da stays positive).
  • The mean is set by the intercept of the returned TraitQTL (BnGStructs.TraitQTL(...; intercept)), not by shifting the effects.
  • t.σₐ is the standard deviation (with the n − 1 divisor) of the genetic values in hap. It includes the covariances between QTL from linkage disequilibrium in that sample; the genic standard deviation, $\sqrt{\sum 2p(1-p)a^2}$, differs from it. trait_summary reports both.

The realised values are checked after scaling and an ArgumentError is thrown if they miss the target by more than atol (relative), or if the QTL are monomorphic in hap.

BnGsim.founder_population — Method
founder_population(hap::Haplotype, qtl::TraitQTL, trait::Trait, key::SimKey) -> Population
founder_population(hap::Haplotype, qtl::MultiTraitQTL, traits, key::SimKey; Re = I) -> Population

Cohort 0 of a breeding program: the individuals of hap, unrelated founders, with sexes drawn at random (Domain.MATING), genetic values from qtl and one phenotype per trait (Domain.PHENOTYPE). Each trait's residual standard deviation is fixed from the trait as $\sigma_e = \sigma_a\sqrt{(1 - h^2)/h^2}$, with $\sigma_a$ the trait's σₐ, so $h^2$ is the heritability in the founders; Re is the residual correlation matrix of several traits. key carries the seed and replicate; its other fields are set per draw.

The covariance components of the evaluations are those of the founders: $G_0 = D\,C\,D$, $D$ the diagonal of the traits' σₐ and $C$ the correlation matrix of the founders' genetic values (exactly the R of a multi-trait eqtl), and $R_0 = D_e R_e D_e$.

Contemporary groups: with herds = H, each founder is placed in one of H herds at random (a keyed draw, Domain.MATING), and every offspring is born in its dam's herd. A record of trait j belongs to the group of the animal's herd and the time step of the record (birth plus the trait's age); each group and trait has an effect $N(0, \sigma^2_{cg,j})$, a keyed draw per herd and step (Domain.PHENOTYPE), added to the phenotype, with $\sigma^2_{cg,j} = c^2_j V_{p,j}$, $V_p = \sigma^2_a/h^2$ (the variance within groups), from c2 (a number for all traits, or one per trait; default 0, no effect). h^2 stays the heritability within groups. Effects of different traits are independent. When some c2 > 0, evaluations fit the groups as fixed effects in place of the overall mean (see evaluate!).

Repeated records: with records = m > 1 for a trait (a number, or one per trait), an animal gets a record of it at every time step it is alive from the trait's age on, at most m, $y = \mu + a + pe + cg + e$: a permanent-environment effect $pe$ drawn at birth (keyed, Domain.PHENOTYPE) and a temporary residual $e$ drawn per record, with $\sigma^2_{pe} = (r - h^2)V_p$ and $\sigma^2_e = (1 - r)V_p$ for the repeatability $r$ (default $h^2$: no permanent environment; one number, or one per trait with nothing for $h^2$). With several traits, the permanent environments are correlated by Rpe (among the traits with $r > h^2$) and the temporary residuals of the records one animal gets at one step by Re, drawn jointly from the animal's stream of that step; single-record traits are then recorded step by step too. The records are made at the start of each step! (records); phenotypes holds the mean of an animal's records so far (NaN before the first). R of covariances is then the covariance of the temporary residuals and PE that of the permanent environments used by the evaluations.

Missing records: missing (a number, or one per trait) is the probability that a record is missing at random, a keyed draw per animal, trait and time step of the record; unrecorded_herds the probability that a herd never records a trait, a keyed draw per herd and trait. A missing record is not made (NaN as phenotype, no entry in records); the values of the other records do not change, as the masks have their own keyed streams.

A trait's sex (0 females, 1 males, 2 both) limits its records to one sex; its age, a whole number of time steps, is the age at which a record becomes available to evaluations (0: at birth). Phenotypes are drawn for every animal and trait either way, from the animal's own keyed stream, so these settings change which records are seen, not the values drawn.

BnGsim.generation_summary — Method
generation_summary(pop::Population, g; weights = ones(k), segs = nothing) -> NamedTuple

For cohort g: its size; mean, variance and genic variance $\sum 2p(1-p)a^2$ at the QTL of the genetic values (one trait), or of the aggregate genotype $H = w'g$ with weights as $w$ (several traits, then also mean_<name>, var_<name> per trait and the genetic correlation rg_<a>_<b> of each pair within the cohort); the QTL with a non-zero effect on that genetic value (on $H$, the effects $w'a$) at which the favourable allele is segregating (qtl_seg), fixed (qtl_fixed) or lost (qtl_lost) in the cohort, and the architecture of the segregating ones: their mean minor allele frequency qtl_maf, qtl_alpha, the slope of $\log a^2$ on $\log 2p(1-p)$ (effect variance $\propto [2p(1-p)]^\alpha$; negative when large effects are rare), and qtl_top10, the share of the genic variance in the largest tenth of them; mean pedigree inbreeding F and mean pedigree coancestry $\bar\theta_g$ over all pairs of the cohort, self-coancestry $(1 + F_i)/2$ included (founders unrelated and non-inbred); the loss of heterozygosity Fhet; the number of animals alive at step g (all cohorts); and, given the founder segments segs (founder_segments(pop, mm), which step! passes when the scheme records meioses), the mean realized inbreeding Fseg of the cohort, the proportion of the genome at which an animal's two haplotypes descend from the same founder haplotype (its expectation is the pedigree F).

Fgrm is the mean genomic inbreeding of the cohort, $G_{ii} - 1$ of VanRaden's (2008) first $G = ZZ'/2\sum p_0(1-p_0)$ with the founders' allele frequencies $p_0$ as base, over all loci segregating in the founders: $Z_{ij} = M_{ij} - 2p_{0j}$ for the dosage $M_{ij}$. As $p_0$ is estimated from the founders, two distinct founder alleles have mean coancestry $-1/(2n_0 - 1)$ on this base, so without selection the expectation of Fgrm is about $F - (1 - F)/(2n_0 - 1)$, $F$ the pedigree inbreeding (checked over 200 replicates of examples/minimal.json with random selection, steps 2–6); in cohort 0 it is the founders' own deviation from Hardy–Weinberg proportions. Fgrm measures the realized homozygosity of each animal, Fhet the loss of heterozygosity of the cohort.

Fhet is $1 - (1 - \bar\theta_0)\,\bar H_g/\bar H_0$ over all loci, with $\bar H$ the mean of $2p(1-p)$ from the cohort's own allele frequencies and $\bar\theta_0 = 1/(2n_0)$ the founders' mean coancestry. Each $\bar H_g$ is a sample estimate whose expectation is $H(1 - \bar\theta_g)$, $H$ the heterozygosity of the population the founders were drawn from, so without selection the expectation of Fhet is the mean coancestry $\bar\theta_g$. Under selection, Fhet − coancestry measures the extra loss of heterozygosity at loci linked to QTL; both carry drift noise that is large when few chromosome segments segregate.

BnGsim.ocs_contributions — Method
ocs_contributions(u, Θ, b, K, male) -> (c, feasible, value)

Contributions c (each sex summing to ½, all $\ge 0$) maximising $u'c$ subject to $f(c) = c'\Theta c + 2b'c \le K$: optimum contribution selection (Meuwissen 1997) with the linear term $b$ that the fixed contributions of older age classes add.

The problem is convex. It is solved exactly by homotopy in the weight $\nu$ of the gain: $c(\nu)$ minimises $f(c) - 2\nu u'c$ under the sex constraints and $c \ge 0$. For a fixed set of zero contributions $c(\nu) = y + \nu x$ is linear. The path starts at $\nu = \infty$, the best male and the best female (ties by position), and $\nu$ is lowered from one breakpoint to the next (a candidate entering when its multiplier reaches zero, or a contribution reaching zero) until $f(c(\nu)) = K$. The free set stays as small as the solution, so a breakpoint costs a factorisation of the order of the number of parents. If the path reaches $\nu = 0$ first, $c(0)$ minimises $f$, the bound cannot be met, and it is returned with feasible = false. value is $f(c)$ at the solution.

BnGsim.resource_estimate — Method
resource_estimate(s::Scenario) -> NamedTuple

The resources scenario s needs, from the scenario alone:

  • peak_rss_bytes: the expected peak resident memory of a run of one replicate at a time, the sum of memory_terms: the process itself, the historical populations held at once, the packed genomes of all animals (and with them the pedigree and single records), the dense relationship matrices of :gblup and :ssgblup, the repeated records, and the work array of report_reliability;
  • peak_rss_bound_bytes: a conservative bound for admission: the expectation times a factor that no calibration or held-out run exceeded, with a margin;
  • disk_bytes: the files bngsim run writes;
  • seconds: a rough run time of all replicates (historical population and breeding loop), on the calibration host of the time model mostly within about ±30 % (up to 2.2 times low on one scenario; calibrated up to 20 000 genotyped animals);
  • time_terms: seconds by part: the historical runs, and over all replicates the founder setup, meiosis, summaries, evaluation (with the G or H inverse), mating, reliabilities, the rest of the loop, and the compilation of the first replicate;
  • size: the ScenarioSize the terms are computed from.

The coefficients are calibrated at one thread count, memory and time on two hosts (see the documentation page "Resource estimate"); memory and time differ on others, and the garbage collector makes the peak of a given run vary by about 10 %. Replicates run one after the other, so memory does not grow with their number.

results_dict(s::Scenario) -> JSON object

The content of s that determines its results, and its SHA-256 (of the compact JSON text, results_sha256): the canonical object (scenario_dict) without the keys that do not change any replicate's results (schema, schema_version, name, replicates, founders.history_dir) and without every key whose value is its default, so that a key added in a later release with a default that keeps earlier results leaves the hash of every earlier scenario unchanged (schema policy, package roadmap §11 b). BnGsim.transport — Method
transport(supply, demand, cost; cap = typemax(Int)) -> Matrix{Int}

The integer flows F (p × q) minimising sum(cost .* F) subject to row sums supply, column sums demand (equal totals) and 0 ≤ F ≤ cap in every cell: a transportation problem, solved exactly by successive shortest paths with potentials (Dijkstra on reduced costs; cost ≥ 0). The priority queue orders by distance then node, so ties go to the lowest-numbered sire or dam and the result is deterministic. Throws an ArgumentError when no flow meets cap.

BnGsim.write_archive — Method
write_archive(dir, pop::Population, vm::VariantMap, sp::Species;
              panel = Int32[], qtl = Int32[], t = current_step(pop)) -> Vector{String}

Write the population pop to directory dir (created if needed) and return the files written:

  • pedigree.csv: id, sire, dam, sex, generation, exit, herd (0 for an unknown parent; sex M or F; generation the birth cohort; exit the first time step at which the animal was dead or culled, empty while it lives; herd, so that the contemporary group of a record of a trait is herd and generation + age);
  • records.csv: id and one column per trait, the phenotype where it was recorded by time step t (expressed in the animal's sex, at or after the trait's age, alive at that age; observed), empty otherwise;
  • truth.csv: id, then tbv_<trait> (the true genetic value) and ebv_<trait> (the last evaluation, empty if none) for each trait;
  • traits.csv: one row per trait: name, h2, sigma_a, mu, sex, age, intercept, c2, records, repeatability (the constant of its genetic value; the variance of the contemporary-group effects as a fraction of sigma_a²/h2; the maximum number of records per animal and the repeatability);
  • repeated.csv (traits with records > 1): id, trait, step, value, one row per record made by time step t; records.csv then holds the mean of an animal's records;
  • qtl.csv (if qtl is given, the locus indices of the QTL in vm): locus, chr, bp, then the additive effect of one copy of the allele coded 1 for each trait;
  • with panel (locus indices in vm, e.g. the chip), the genotypes of every animal whose genome is kept at those loci, in PLINK 1 binary format: genotypes.bed (SNP-major), genotypes.bim (chr, the id l<locus>, the position in centimorgans within the chromosome from the map or, without one, from the species' genetic length, the position in bp within the chromosome, the allele coded 1 as A1 and the other as A2) and genotypes.fam (family 1, the animal id, sire, dam, sex 1/2, phenotype -9).

Numbers are written in Julia's shortest round-trip form. Animals keep the ids of the population. In the .bed file the allele coded 1 is A1, the fifth column of the .bim: PLINK 1.9 counts it with --recode A --keep-allele-order (without that flag it makes A1 the minor allele of the sample), plink2 with --export A --export-allele on that column (by default it counts the sixth). BnGsim.Legacy

The building blocks of BnGsim 0.1–0.3: a recombination and gene-drop toolkit (sum_map, BnGsim.Legacy.crossovers(sp, vm), gamete!, gene_drop!, random_mate, rnd_ped, expand, sample_id), trait helpers (norm_mv!, tbv!, phenotype!, dummy_traits), founder sampling (FounderPopulation, sample_founder_population), historical populations (HistoricalSpec, historical_population, continue_population, read_packed_haplotypes, write_xy), vanraden_g and the text provenance header.

struct HistoricalSpec

A historical (pre-founder) population run with FisherWright.fisher_wright.

Fields

continue_population(res::FisherWrightResult, ns, mr; M = 1e8, mut_base = 1e8,
                    rng = Random.default_rng()) -> FisherWrightResult

Continue a FisherWright population for length(ns) further generations with census size ns[g] in generation g, e.g. a decline from the historical size to a breed-like size before founders are sampled. This is fisher_wright(...; init = res, popsize = ns): the same model (dioecious Wright–Fisher, Poisson crossovers at 1/M per bp, infinite-sites mutation at mr / mut_base per bp per meiosis) with a census size that may change every generation.
