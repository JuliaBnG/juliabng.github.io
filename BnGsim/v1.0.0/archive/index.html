Archives

An archive holds what other software needs to analyse a simulated population: the pedigree, the records, the true and estimated breeding values, the QTL with their effects, and genotypes. bngsim run ... --archive PANEL writes one per replicate to DIR/archive/r<N>/ when the replicate finishes, and write_archive writes one from Julia.

The files and their columns are listed in Output files: pedigree.csv (with the herd), records.csv, truth.csv, traits.csv, repeated.csv (with repeated records), qtl.csv, and PLINK 1 binary genotypes genotypes.bed/.bim/.fam of every animal whose genome is kept, at the loci of PANEL.

PANEL is chip (the scenario's chip), qtl, all (every segregating locus of the founders) or none (no genotypes). The archives are listed with their SHA-256 in provenance.json, and a stopped and resumed run writes the same files, byte for byte.

In genotypes.bed the allele coded 1 is A1, the fifth column of genotypes.bim. PLINK 1.9 counts it with --recode A --keep-allele-order; without that flag it recodes A1 as the minor allele of the sample. plink2 counts it with --export A --export-allele on that column, and by default counts the sixth. Both tools were checked to read back the simulated genotypes exactly (200 animals, 300 loci).

Infinitesimal breeding values

The breeding loop simulates finite loci. To test software on data whose genetic model is exactly the animal model, infinitesimal draws $u \sim N(0, G_0 \otimes A)$ and phenotypes $y = \mu + u + e$, $e \sim N(0, R_0)$, on any pedigree, by Mendelian sampling: each animal gets the mean of its parents' values plus a term with covariance $(1 - \tfrac14(1 + F_s) - \tfrac14(1 + F_d)) G_0$. Every animal draws from its own SimKey stream, so its values do not depend on the order of computation. The test suite checks the covariances against $G_0 \otimes A$ on a pedigree with inbred animals.

using BnGsim
r = run_replicate(read_scenario("examples/minimal.json"))
ped = pedigree(r.population)
u, y = infinitesimal(ped.sire, ped.dam, [1.0;;], [3.0;;], SimKey(1))
