Resource estimate

resource_estimate and bngsim estimate predict, from the scenario alone, the peak resident memory of a run, a conservative bound on it, the disk the command line writes, and a rough run time. bngsim run --memory-limit SIZE refuses a scenario whose bound exceeds SIZE before it starts (exit code 5), so that a run fails cleanly instead of being killed by the operating system (roadmap invariant I7).

The model

Peak memory is the sum of four terms, each a fitted multiple of a size computed from the scenario (ScenarioSize):

TermSizeFitted bytes per byte
processBnGsim loaded and compiled577 MB
historical populationone copy of its haplotypes, $8N\,\bar m$ with $\bar m = \mu L \min(t, 4N)$ derived alleles per haplotype5.42
genomesthe packed genomes of all animals, $2 \cdot n_{animals} \cdot S / 8$, with $S = 4N\mu L \ln(1 + 2 n_{id} t / 4N)$ segregating loci among the founders4.15
dense evaluationone $n_g \times n_g$ matrix, $8 n_g^2$, for the genotyped animals13.3 (:gblup), 19.0 (:ssgblup), one trait; more for several traits and REML (below)

$\bar m$ and $S$ follow from neutral theory for a population that starts without variation: $\mu L$ new alleles per haplotype per generation until fixation balances them near $4N\mu L$, and the total branch length of the founders' genealogy truncated at the start of the run. On the calibration scenarios they matched the realized values within 4 %. The multiples count the copies the code holds at its peak (buffers of the historical run; the relationship matrix, its blend with A, its factor and inverse and the mixed-model equations of GBLUP), plus memory not yet collected. Terms for the pedigree and for the dense cohort matrix of the per-step summary were tried and fitted to zero.

The bound is the expected peak times 1.3: no calibration or held-out run exceeded the expectation by more than 15.3 %.

The run time has its own model, by phase of the run (below, "Run time").

Calibration of the memory model

The memory coefficients were fitted on one host: AMD Threadripper 3970X, Linux x86_64, Julia 1.13.4, 8 threads, BnGsim 0.14. Sixteen calibration scenarios cover the historical population ($N$ 500 to 3,000, $t$ 500 to 4,000), PBLUP with 6,000 to 41,000 animals, and GBLUP and ssGBLUP with 2,000 to 8,000 genotyped animals; six held-out scenarios, not used in the fit, vary the genome, the number of traits, the genotyped cohorts and the historical size, and include examples/minimal.json. Each ran twice in a fresh process. The garbage collector makes the peak of one scenario vary between runs by up to 25 % (GBLUP with 8,000 animals: 4,512 and 5,761 MB), so the expectation is fitted to the mean of the two runs.

The mean of every scenario lies within 15 % of its estimate (calibration within 11.4 %, held out within 14.3 %), and examples/minimal.json, the roadmap's smoke scenario, within 10 %. The test suite checks the roadmap's gate on these measurements: every mean within 25 % of the estimate and no run above the bound.

On another host, Julia version or thread count the coefficients change: on a 12-core desktop (AMD Ryzen 9 3900X) at 4 threads the same scenarios peaked 6 % to 28 % lower than on the calibration host at 8 (GBLUP with 6,000 genotyped animals: 2,536 MB against about 3,500 MB), so there the estimate errs high, on the safe side. The scripts to measure and refit are in validation/estimate/ (run.sh, measure.jl, fit.jl).

Several traits and REML (0.37)

The calibration of 0.15 had one trait and no REML. With $k$ traits the mixed-model equations of GBLUP and ssGBLUP are $k n_g$ square, and REML holds further matrices of that size, so the dense term is $8 n_g^2$ times

\[c + t\,(k^2 - 1) + r\,k^2\,[\text{REML}],\]

with $c$ the one-trait multiple above, $t = 9.83$ (:gblup) and $15.1$ (:ssgblup), and $r = 9.85$. Until 0.36 the term ignored $k$ and REML, and the peaks of such runs exceeded the bound: up to 1.85 times with two traits and 3.06 times with two traits under REML (amd, 8 threads). $t$ and $r$ are fitted to the peaks of the time calibration (below): three scenarios with GBLUP, two (so exactly) with ssGBLUP; REML with ssGBLUP uses the GBLUP value of $r$. Five held-out scenarios in validation/estimate/check/ (three traits; REML with one trait, and with two traits under ssGBLUP; repeated records with GBLUP; three traits with repeated records) had mean peaks 0.94 to 1.13 times their expectation. Over all 56 scenarios of the time calibration the largest peak is 0.96 times its bound (the table at the end of the fit below). These peaks were measured on the time calibration host, the one-trait multiples on the memory calibration host; on the one-trait scenarios the two agree within the 15 % of the memory calibration.

Later terms (0.33)

Features added after the calibration contribute terms computed from their data structures, checked against measured runs rather than fitted to many (scenarios and runs in validation/estimate/later/):

  • several shared histories (founders.histories): the history being simulated as before, and each further one held as a result at 2.4 bytes per byte of its haplotypes (fitted to the one scenario with four);
  • a census trajectory (founders.popsize): the history term uses the largest census, since FisherWright sizes its buffers by it, and N in the allele and locus counts the harmonic mean of the census; the run time sums the work of every generation at its census;
  • repeated records: 40 bytes per record;
  • report_reliability: its work array, 64 columns per trait of the mixed-model equations, and one solve per candidate in the run time.

Run time

The run time (seconds, and by part time_terms) is the sum of the phases of a run, each a fitted multiple of a size computed from the scenario (_time_features), summed over the time steps $g = 0, \dots, T-1$ at which the loop evaluates and selects, when the pedigree holds $n_{id} + g\,n_{off}$ animals:

PartSize, per replicate
historyalleles carried through meiosis in each historical run, about $2N\mu L \sum_g \min(g, 4N)$ (per history simulated), and the same times $\log_2$ of the haplotypes of one generation over 16 MB (zero below)
setupfounder haplotypes × segregating loci, $2 n_{id} S$
meiosisoffspring haplotypes × loci, $2 T n_{off} S$
summarycohort and founder haplotypes × loci (allele counts), and animals × cohort (pedigree relationships)
evaluationequations and equations × $k$ (sparse PBLUP), $(k\,n_g)^2$ and $(k\,n_g)^3$ (dense GBLUP, ssGBLUP), equations and $(k\,n_g)^3$ again under REML, repeated records; and the G or H inverse, $n_g^2$ × chip and $n_g^3$, separately for ssGBLUP
matinganimals × parents (minimum-coancestry mating)
reliabilitycandidates × equations (report_reliability)
restper step, and per animal and step
compilethe first replicate: a cost per feature used (BLUP, dense G, ssGBLUP, several traits, repeated records, REML, minimum-coancestry mating)

with $S$ the segregating loci, $k$ the traits and $n_g$ the genotyped animals at the step (all evaluated animals with :gblup, the last genotyped_cohorts cohorts with :ssgblup).

Calibration of the time model (0.37)

The time model is calibrated on a different host from the memory model: an AMD Ryzen 9 5950X (16 cores, 93 GB), Linux x8664, Julia 1.13.4, 8 threads, BnGsim 0.37, otherwise idle. `validation/estimate/runphases.shruns every scenario ofscenarios/,later/,phases/andcheck/and the examples, 56 in all, in two processes; each process runs replicate 1 (cold, with compilation) and replicate 2 (warm), each with its own history, timing every phase (measurephases.jl,phases.tsv).fitphases.jlfits each phase to the mean warm time of its scenarios by non-negative least squares on relative error (runs under 0.2 s counted at 0.2 s), and the compilation to the cold-minus-warm difference. The examples, the held-out scenariosv1–v6of the memory calibration andcheck/` are held out.

On every scenario over 1 s but one, measured warm time per replicate was 0.77 to 1.28 times the model on the calibration scenarios, and 0.88 to 1.49 on the held-out ones (dairy.json 1.05, sheep.json 1.26). The lowest, 0.77, is GBLUP with a 20 000-locus chip: forming G costs less per locus with long chips than the linear term assumes, so the estimate is high there. The largest held-out misses are REML (1.49 with one trait under GBLUP, 1.24 with two under ssGBLUP): REML's cost is its number of iterations, which depends on the data, not on the size. The exception is l_records (three traits, two with repeated records, 3000 offspring per step for ten steps): 2.2 times the model, all in the evaluation, whose cost per equation is 5.7 µs against 0.6 to 2.1 µs in every other PBLUP scenario; a smaller scenario of the same kind (check/c_rep3_pblup.json) is at 0.99, so this is the solver's convergence on that scenario rather than a missing size term. Compilation is modelled to within about 1 s, worse under REML, whose cold-minus-warm difference also carries the difference in iterations between the two replicates.

Limits. The calibration reaches 20 000 genotyped animals (GBLUP and ssGBLUP) and $k n_g = 20 000$ (two traits); beyond, the estimate extrapolates its cubic terms. In 0.37.0 these had fitted to zero, because every scenario up to 13 000 genotyped animals had the same chip, so that forming G ($n_g^2$ × chip) and inverting it ($n_g^3$) could not be told apart; with 20 000 genotyped animals and a 1000-locus chip the inverse alone took 21 s and 0.37.0 estimated 3 s. 0.37.1 adds scenarios up to 20 000 genotyped animals with chips of 1000, 5000 and 20 000 loci: the inverse costs $1.66 \times 10^{-12}$ s per $n_g^3$ (about 600 GFLOPS for the $n_g^3$ operations of inv(cholesky(G)); BLAS chooses its own threads, independently of Julia's), and the dense equations a further $2.2 \times 10^{-13}$ s per $(k n_g)^3$. The run time depends on the host and on the number of threads; on others it is a relative guide only. The history term depends on the processor caches.
