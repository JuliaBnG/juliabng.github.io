Validation of selection

Selection in fisher_wright (fitness = SiteFitness(loci)) is checked by the fixation probability of one new copy of a selected allele, which depends on every part of the mechanism: the fitness of each genotype, the draw of parents in proportion to fitness, and the transmission of the selected allele through meiosis.

Model

  • $N$ diploid individuals, discrete non-overlapping generations, two sexes. Sex is Binomial($N$, ½) per generation, conditioned on both sexes being present, or exactly $N/2$ males where stated.
  • One selected site; a genotype has fitness $1$, $1 + hs$ or $1 + s$ with zero, one or two copies of the derived allele.
  • Soft selection within each sex: every offspring draws its sire among the males and its dam among the females with probability proportional to fitness, with replacement.
  • A replicate starts from one copy on a random haplotype of a population without other variation, has no further mutation, and runs to loss or fixation of the allele.

Reference

Kimura's diffusion formula and the exact Markov chain of an ideal Wright–Fisher population describe a single pool of $2N$ genes. The two-sex model differs from that by terms of order $1/N$:

  • the binomial sex ratio lowers the variance effective size to about $4N_mN_f/N$, and the probability of fixation of a favoured allele is proportional to $N_e/N$;
  • a rare allele competes only within the sex of its carrier, where its frequency is twice its frequency in the population, so its advantage over the mean of its competitors is reduced by about $s/(2N)$ more than in a single pool.

The reference for the implementation is therefore an independent simulation of the same model, fixation-reference.jl. It follows one locus as genotype counts and shares no code with the package: sex by coin flips, parents by rejection sampling on fitness, one Bernoulli draw per transmitted gene. The ideal Wright–Fisher value (wf_exact, the exact chain for $2N$ genes) is printed beside it.

Recurrent mutation at many sites

The fixation probability checks one new allele at one site. validate-selection-balance.jl checks the full multi-site path of fisher_wright: mutation at the fitness loci, selection on the product of fitness over sites, and the extraction of selected substitutions.

Design. $N = 30$, $K$ selected sites, each on its own chromosome of 100 bp, so that the sites recombine freely and the neutral process is switched off. The population starts without variation and runs for $T = 300$ generations ($10N$); at the end, each site is classified by its derived-allele count, with substituted sites in the class "fixed". The reference is a naive simulation of one site of the same model (the mutation probability per gene and generation, $1 - e^{-\mu}$, follows from the engine's Poisson hits). Sites in one population share its pedigree, so the unit of replication of the engine is the run; standard errors are taken over runs.

Weak selection at large N: Poisson random field

The checks above compare the engine with exact simulations of the same model at $N = 30$. validate-prf.jl compares it with diffusion theory at large $N$ and weak selection: the stationary site frequency spectrum of the Poisson random field (Sawyer and Hartl 1992).

Design. $N = 1{,}000$ (binomial sex, $N_e = 1/a + ½ = 999.5$), run for $T = 20N$ generations from a population without variation, so that $e^{-10}$ of the slowest neutral mode is left. There are $K = 500{,}000$ selected sites, evenly spaced on 20 chromosomes of 1 Morgan, in five interleaved classes of 100,000 sites with $\alpha = 2N_e s \in \{-10, -2, 0, 2, 10\}$ and $h = ½$. Mutation is $4N\mu = 10^{-4}$ per site, and neutral mutation is off.

Infinite sites. The fitness loci are finite: a fixed site stays fixed, and a hit on a carried site has no effect. The low per-site rate keeps recurrent hits at about $10^{-4}$ of the spectrum. At most 0.44% of the sites fix ($\alpha = 10$), so the influx is that of the sites not fixed at the end, $K_a$, to well under 0.1% over the lifetime of the variants seen. About 400 sites segregate at a time, so interference between them is negligible.

Statistic and reference. For each run and class, the expected spectrum $\xi_i$ of a sample of $n = 20$ genes, drawn without replacement from the $2N$, is computed exactly (hypergeometric) from the derived-allele counts. It is divided by $K_a$ and by $\theta = 4N_e\mu$, and summed over bins of $i$. The reference is

\[\frac{\xi_i}{\theta} = \int_0^1 \binom{n}{i} x^i (1 - x)^{n-i} \frac{1 - e^{-\alpha(1-x)}}{(1 - e^{-\alpha})\,x(1 - x)}\,dx,\]

with $1/i$ for $\alpha = 0$. This is the diffusion limit for genic selection, $\Delta x = (s/2)x(1 - x)$, with drift variance $x(1 - x)/(2N_e)$. Its error at $N = 1{,}000$ is of order $1/N$. The unit of replication is the run: 1,000 runs per seed, standard errors over runs, and failure at $|z| > 3.5$.

Selection on a trait

validate-trait.jl checks TraitFitness against quantitative-genetic theory. The mechanism itself, that one generation of selection moves the mean genotypic value by exactly the fitness-weighted mean of the parents within each sex, is tested exactly in CI ("Selection on a trait").

Neutral genetic variance. $K = 20{,}000$ additive loci ($h = ½$, so the allele substitution effect is $\alpha = a/2$) with effects $a \sim N(0, 0.2^2)$ drawn anew for every replicate, mutation $\mu = 10^{-5}$ per site ($U = K\mu = 0.2$ per haplotype and generation), constant fitness, a population without variation at the start. For allele indicators, the sample variance of the dosage among the $N$ individuals has expectation $2H_B - H_W$; with independent effects of zero mean the loci add, so

\[E[\operatorname{var} G] = E[\alpha^2]\,(2H_B - H_W),\]

with $H_B$ and $H_W$ from the two-sex recursion with $2U$ new alleles per pair of genes (infinite sites). The expectation is exact at every generation, not only at equilibrium, where it is $2N_eV_m$ with $N_e = 1/a$ and $V_m = 2UE[\alpha^2]$ (Lynch and Hill 1986). With $\sigma_e > 0$, also $E[\operatorname{var} P] = E[\operatorname{var} G] + \sigma_e^2$.

Directional response, for comparison. With fitness $e^{\beta P}$ and Gaussian $G$ and $P$, the fitness-weighted mean of $G$ is shifted by $\beta V_G$ and its variance is unchanged (no Bulmer effect), so the response per generation is about $\beta V_G$. In a finite population the weighted mean over $n$ parents of a sex is a ratio estimator, with a first-order bias of $-\beta V_G e^{\beta^2 V_P}/n$.

Pleiotropy

validate-pleiotropy.jl checks TraitFitness with two traits on the same loci. The mechanism is tested exactly in CI ("Pleiotropy"): one generation of selection moves the mean genotypic value of each trait, the one not selected directly included, by exactly the fitness-weighted mean of the parents within each sex; fitness is the function of the phenotype vector; identical columns of effects give identical genotypic records; and the environmental covariance matrix is $\Sigma_e$.

Neutral genetic covariance matrix. As for one trait, with $K = 20{,}000$ additive loci and the effects of each site on the two traits drawn from $N(0, \Sigma_a)$, $\Sigma_a = 0.2^2 \begin{pmatrix}1 & \rho\\ \rho & 1\end{pmatrix}$, $\rho = -0.5$, anew for every replicate. The sample covariance of the dosages of one site has expectation $2H_B - H_W$, and with effects independent across sites the sites add, so

\[E[\operatorname{cov}(G_s, G_t)] = E[\alpha_s\alpha_t]\,(2H_B - H_W), \qquad E[\alpha_s\alpha_t] = \Sigma_{a,st}/4,\]

for every generation from a population without variation: the covariance of pleiotropic effects becomes the genetic covariance in proportion. With an environmental covariance $\Sigma_e$, also $E[S_P - S_G] = \Sigma_e$ for the sample covariance matrices.

Direct and correlated response, for comparison. With fitness $e^{\beta' P}$, $\beta = (\beta_1, 0)$, and Gaussian $G$ and $P$, the fitness-weighted mean of $G$ is shifted by $\mathbf{G}\beta$ (Lande 1979): the second trait responds by $\beta_1 G_{21}$ per generation, through the shared loci only. The script compares the mean cumulative response with the mean of $\sum_g \mathbf{G}_g\beta$ (a ratio of means: $\sum_g G_{21}$ of one replicate can be near zero), with the same first-order finite-$N$ bias of the weighted mean as for one trait.

Sex-specific fitness

validate-sex-specific.jl checks SexSpecific at one autosomal site, $N = 10{,}000$, every individual heterozygous at the start, no mutation. Genotypes $AA$, $Aa$ and $aa$ have fitness $1 + s$, $1 + hs$ and 1, with $(s_m, h_m)$ in males and $(s_f, h_f)$ in females. With soft selection within each sex, the frequency of $A$ in the gametes of the selected males is

\[x' = \frac{P_{AA}(1 + s_m) + P_{Aa}(1 + h_m s_m)/2}{\bar w_m},\]

and $y'$ likewise for the females, from the genotype frequencies $P$ of the parents (the same in both sexes, since sex is independent of genotype); the offspring have $P_{AA} = x'y'$ and $P_{Aa} = x'(1 - y') + y'(1 - x')$, and the expected frequency $(x' + y')/2$ (Owen 1953; Kidwell et al. 1977). This is exact in expectation for one generation given the parents, which CI tests ("Sex-specific fitness"), as it tests that sires are weighted by the male and dams by the female fitness under each mating scheme. Over $t$ generations drift erodes $pq$ and the mean of a finite population lags the deterministic trajectory by order $s\,pq\,t^2/(8N)$, here at most about $8 \times 10^{-4}$.

The four cases are sexually antagonistic additive selection ($s_m = 0.1$, $s_f = -0.1$); selection in males only ($s_m = 0.1$) and in both sexes at half strength ($s = 0.05$), the same to first order in $s$; and antagonistic selection with dominance reversal ($s_m = 0.2$, $h_m = 0.8$, $s_f = -0.2$, $h_f = 0.2$), which keeps the allele polymorphic.

Stabilizing selection: matched SLiM model

validate-stabilizing.jl checks the genetic variance maintained by mutation, Gaussian stabilizing selection and drift (TraitFitness with Stabilizing). The theory for this balance is approximate, so the test reference is a matched model in SLiM (validate-stabilizing.slim), an independent implementation with SLiM's own meiosis and mutation; the diffusion theory is reported alongside for comparison.

Model. $N = 500$ diploids. In every generation the roles of sire and dam are assigned anew (binomial sex, conditioned on both; $N_e = 499.5$), and every offspring draws its sire among the males and its dam among the females in proportion to fitness, with replacement. $P = G + e$, $e \sim N(0, 1)$ drawn anew, fitness $\exp(-P^2/2\omega^2)$ with $\omega^2 = 49$ and the optimum at 0. There are $K = 8{,}000$ additive trait loci ($h = ½$), evenly spaced on 20 chromosomes of 1 Morgan, in four interleaved classes of 2,000 with substitution effect $|\alpha|$ fixed per class and a sign drawn anew for each run. The sizes give $B = 2N_e\alpha^2/V_s \approx 1, 4, 16, 64$ at $V_s = \omega^2 + V_P \approx 51$. Mutation to the derived allele is at $4N\mu = 10^{-3}$ per site; the sites are biallelic, with no back mutation, and a fixed site stays fixed (SLiM: stacking policy "f", no conversion to substitutions). Neutral mutation is off. SLiM runs the model as nonWF with explicit reproduction, so that the sexes, the phenotypes and the weighted draw are coded exactly as above.

Protocol and statistics. From a population without variation, $20N$ generations of burn-in, then 21 samples $2N$ apart (30,000 generations per run). Per sample: the genic variance $V_a = \sum 2pq\alpha^2$ of each class, divided by the number of sites not fixed, $K_a$, and by $4\mu V_s$; the fixed sites per class; the mean genotypic value; and the means of $V_P$, $V_G$ and the variance of log fitness over the preceding window. The samples of a run are averaged and standard errors taken over 2,000 runs per simulator and seed. A comparison of the two simulators fails at $|z| > 3.5$; the mean $G$ is also compared with 0, its exact expectation under random signs.

Theory, for comparison. Forward mutation at biallelic loci makes every allele unique: this is the house-of-cards family (Turelli 1984; Bürger 2000), not the Gaussian regime (Kimura 1965; Lande 1975), which needs a continuum of alleles per locus. With the mean at the optimum a site is underdominant, $\Delta x = -(\alpha^2/2V_s)x(1-x)(1-2x)$ (Robertson 1956). The single-locus diffusion gives the expected genic variance per site, in units of $4\mu V_s$,

\[B \int_0^1 \frac{S(1) - S(x)}{S(1)\,\psi(x)}\,dx, \qquad \psi(x) = e^{Bx(1-x)},\quad S(x) = \int_0^x \psi,\]

which is $B/2$ (neutral) for $B \to 0$ and 1 (Turelli's house of cards) for $B \to \infty$, and above 1 in between. Bürger's stochastic house-of-cards formula, $(B/2)/(1 + B/2)$ in these units, is printed too. The diffusion leaves out the linkage disequilibrium that selection builds: carriers of an allele of effect $\alpha$ sit on backgrounds shifted by about $-\alpha k/(1 + k)$, $k = V_G/V_s$, which weakens selection on the allele to about $B(1 - k)^2$. The table prints the diffusion with this correction ("LD-corr."). Neither includes the drift of the mean about the optimum.

Drift under FamilySize with selection: the pedigree

The drift test of FamilySize (see Validation of the mating schemes) uses the recursion of the heterozygosity, which holds without selection only. With selection the reference is the pedigree itself. validate-family-selection.jl checks that the alleles FisherWright transmits and the pedigree it records agree, generation after generation, under FamilySize with and without selection.

Reference. At a neutral locus unlinked to every selected locus, the expected heterozygosity of generation $t$ given the pedigree is

\[E[H_t \mid \text{pedigree}] = H^*\,(1 - \bar\theta_t),\]

where $H^*$ is the heterozygosity of the base from which the founders' alleles are drawn and $\bar\theta_t$ is the mean coancestry of generation $t$ over all ordered pairs of individuals, self-coancestry $(1 + F_i)/2$ included. Mendelian segregation at an unlinked locus is independent of the genotypes that selection acts on, so this holds exactly for any selection. That includes the cumulative effect of heritable fitness on drift (Robertson 1961), which acts through the pedigree. $\bar\theta_t$ is computed by the tabular method from the recorded pedigree (pedigree = 10 in each of ten continued runs of 10 generations).

Design. $N = 100$ diploids for 100 generations, with binomial sex. Selection is through TraitFitness: 400 additive loci ($h = ½$, effect $\pm 0.5$, the sign drawn per locus and run) on chromosomes 1–4, mutating at $\mu = 10^{-3}$ per locus and generation from a monomorphic start, with $\sigma_e = 1$. The neutral markers are 1,000 loci on chromosomes 5–24, with the founders' alleles drawn independently at frequency ½ ($H^* = ½$) and no mutation. Every chromosome is 100 Mb and 1 Morgan, so the markers are unlinked to the trait loci. The four scenarios are:

MatingFitness

2FamilySize(dams = :random)Directional(0.5)

Per run, every 10 generations: $H_t$, the mean of $2pq$ over the markers, and $\bar\theta_t$. The test is that the mean over runs of $H_t - ½(1 - \bar\theta_t)$ is 0, with standard errors over 2,000 runs per scenario and seed and failure at $|z| > 3.5$. Reported for comparison are the drift effective size from $\bar\theta$ between generations 50 and 100, and effective_size, the within-generation value from the realized family sizes.

CI test

The testset "Selection under recurrent mutation" runs case 1 of the balance check with 1,000 runs against the reference values (about 10 s on one thread); a dominance of $h = 0.1$ instead of ½ is 5.3 standard errors away.

The testset "Fixation probability under selection" runs four of the fixation ($N = 30$; $s = 0.2$ with $h$ = 0, ½ and 1, and $s = 0$) with 4,000 replicates each and compares them with the reference values of the first table. The three values for $s = 0.2$ are far apart, so an error in the fitness of the heterozygote would be caught. Other CI tests check the parts separately: the fitness of an individual against the product over a dense genotype, the joint meiosis of neutral and selected alleles against the recombinant of their union, the mutation rate at the fitness loci, and the weighted draw of parents.

Not covered

  • A theoretical prediction of the cumulative effect of selection on drift (Robertson 1961; Santiago and Caballero 1995): the drift of FamilySize with selection is checked against the realized pedigree, and its effective size is reported, not compared with a formula.
  • Stabilizing selection in the Gaussian regime (a continuum of alleles per locus): the loci are biallelic, so only the house-of-cards regime is compared, against SLiM; the diffusion theory is not tested.
  • A distribution of fitness effects rather than a few fixed values of $s$, and dominance other than ½ at large $N$.
  • Multivariate stabilizing selection (Stabilizing(θ, Ω) with two or more traits) beyond the exact one-generation check in CI: no equilibrium of the genetic covariance matrix is compared with theory or with SLiM.
  • Strong interference between selected sites (Hill–Robertson effects). The effect of selected sites on linked neutral diversity is compared with SLiM on Validation of linked selection.
