Validation of an admixture pulse

FisherWright has no model of subdivision. A single admixture pulse is built from separate calls through init, as described in Merging populations (admixture). This page checks that recipe against two expectations that hold for any neutral pulse: the ancestry proportion and the decay of linkage disequilibrium (LD).

Model

  • N = 500 diploids in every population, with two sexes (sex is Binomial(N, ½) in each generation, conditioned on both sexes) and random mating.
  • 2 chromosomes of 50 Mb at 1e8 bp per Morgan, which is 0.5 M each. Crossovers are Poisson, so Haldane's map $r = (1 - e^{-2d})/2$ holds exactly. Chromosomes recombine freely, and μ = 1e-8 per bp.
  • Ancestral population: 10N = 5000 generations from no variation.
  • Split: populations A and B each start from the ancestral haplotypes and evolve for 100 generations with mutation, in separate calls with result = false.
  • Merge: 150 individuals drawn at random from A and 350 from B, as whole individuals, so α = 0.3. A second variant shuffles the 1000 merged haplotypes among individuals.
  • Continuation: 20 generations at N = 500 with mutate = false, one call per generation, so the same sites are followed throughout.
  • Sites: polymorphic in the merged population with a minor-allele frequency of at least 0.05, thinned evenly to 1000 per chromosome. All statistics are computed on the whole population.

Every replicate has its own ancestral history. The two variants share the history and the merged individuals within a replicate, so each check is within one variant.

References

Ancestry proportion. Let $p_A$ and $p_B$ be the allele frequencies of the merged individuals from A and B, and $\delta = p_A - p_B$. The least-squares estimate is

\[\hat\alpha_t = \frac{\sum (p_t - p_B)\,\delta}{\sum \delta^2}.\]

It equals α at the merge exactly. For later t, $E[\hat\alpha_t] = \alpha$, because drift without mutation or selection leaves $E[p_t] = p_0$.

LD decay. Let $D_t$ be the LD of a pair of sites at recombination fraction r, and $C_0$ the covariance of the two sites between the two haplotypes of one individual at the merge:

\[C_0 = \frac{1}{2N}\sum_i (x_{i1} y_{i2} + x_{i2} y_{i1}) - p_x p_y .\]

A gamete is parental with probability $1 - r$. Otherwise it joins the alleles of the parent's two haplotypes. This gives $E[D_1] = \lambda[(1 - r) D_0 + r C_0]$. From generation 1 on, individuals are random unions of gametes, so $E[D_{t+1}] = \lambda(1 - r)E[D_t]$. Together:

\[E[D_t] = \lambda^t (1 - r)^{t-1}\,[(1 - r) D_0 + r C_0], \qquad t \ge 1,\]

where $\lambda = 1 - 1/(2N_e)$ and $N_e$ comes from the two-sex recursion of Population genetics validation.

  • Whole individuals: $C_0$ is about $\alpha(1-\alpha)\delta_x\delta_y$, the between-source part of $D_0$. That part therefore does not decay in the first generation, whose offspring are F1.
  • Shuffled haplotypes: $C_0 \approx 0$, and the usual $\lambda^t(1 - r)^t D_0$ holds.

For every distance bin, the check compares $S_t = \sum D_t D_0$ with $P_t = \sum E[D_t] D_0$ over the pairs in the bin. Since $E[S_t \mid \text{merge}] = P_t$, the difference $S_t - P_t$ has mean zero over replicates. The prediction neglects terms of order $r/N$ in the drift factor.

Each comparison is a z-score over replicates, and a check fails when |z| > 3.5. A run has 84 checks: 2 variants × 6 times × (1 ancestry + 6 distance bins). If they were independent, the chance that at least one fails by chance would be about 4%. They are not independent: the times of one replicate share its history. The resolution section gives the z-score of the prediction that ignores $C_0$, for the merge of whole individuals.

200 replicates take about 30 minutes on 16 threads (AMD, 16 cores):

julia -t 16 validate-admixture.jl [reps=40] [seed=2026]

Reading the results

  • All checks pass. All 84 checks pass for both seeds. The largest |z| is 3.30 (seed 2026, α̂ at t = 10) and 1.80 (seed 7).
  • Ancestry proportion. α̂ equals α at the merge to rounding (≤ 2e-16). Afterwards it moves with drift and shows no consistent bias. With seed 2026, the merge of whole individuals drifts below 0.3 (z = −3.30 at t = 10), while the shuffled merge drifts above it. With seed 7, both variants stay within 1.8 SE. The six times of one replicate share its drift, so a run that wanders off does so at all later times together.
  • C₀ is the between-source LD. For unlinked pairs, the regression of C₀ on D₀ is 0.592 and 0.593 for the merge of whole individuals. The between-source part α(1 − α)δδ′ gives 0.593 and 0.595. For shuffled haplotypes it is −0.005 and −0.001. The between-source share of D₀ for unlinked pairs is therefore about 0.59, and this is the part that a merge of whole individuals keeps through the F1.
  • LD decay. S/P is between 0.994 and 1.004 in every bin up to t = 3, for both variants and both seeds. At larger t and r, the predicted LD is a small fraction of D₀ (at t = 10 and r ≈ 0.3, 0.7⁹ ≈ 4%), so the ratio becomes noisy, and the unlinked ratios after t = 5 carry no information. There is a weak tendency for S/P to fall below 1 for r > 0.1 and t ≥ 3, by about 1% at t = 5. It has the same sign in both seeds, and its largest |z| is 2.79. It is of the order of the terms of order r/N that the prediction neglects, but it has not been derived here. It is too small to matter for the use of the recipe.
  • Resolution. For the merge of whole individuals, the prediction without C₀ is rejected at t = 1 by z = 87 and 79 for unlinked pairs, 63 and 62 for 0.2–0.5 M, and 24 and 25 for 0.05–0.1 M. The lag of one generation is therefore well resolved. Below 0.01 M, where r is under 1%, the two predictions cannot be told apart.

Not covered

  • Selection: plain haplotypes carry no selected alleles, so a merge is neutral. Histories run with fitness are not merged.
  • Continued gene flow: exchanging migrants between one-generation calls is possible but not checked here.
  • More than two sources, or several pulses: these follow from the same recipe, but are not checked.
  • Estimators of real data: the ancestry estimate uses the true source frequencies of the merged individuals. Methods such as ADMIXTURE, which estimate them, are a question for the data, not for the simulator.
