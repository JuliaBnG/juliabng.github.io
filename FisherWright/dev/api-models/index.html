API reference: population models

The models passed to fisher_wright through its mating, popsize and fitness keywords.

Mating

FisherWright.RandomMating — Type
RandomMating(; nmale = nothing, sexratio = nothing)

Random mating between the sexes: each offspring draws its sire uniformly from the males and its dam uniformly from the females of the parental cohort, with replacement. Sex is assigned anew to every cohort when it reproduces, by one of three models:

  • RandomMating(): Bernoulli(½) per individual, conditioned on both sexes being present, so the number of males among N parents is Binomial(N, ½) truncated to 1:N-1. This is the default of fisher_wright.
  • RandomMating(nmale = m): exactly m males and N - m females, the males being a uniformly random subset of the cohort. Every parental cohort must have more than m individuals.
  • RandomMating(sexratio = p): exactly round(Int, p * N) males, limited to 1:N-1, for a proportion of males 0 < p < 1; the number of males then follows a changing census size.

With N_m males and N_f females the effective size of that generation is approximately 4 N_m N_f / (N_m + N_f) (Wright 1931).

source
FisherWright.FamilySize — Type
FamilySize(; dams = :random, sires = :random, nsires = nothing, ndams = nothing,
           nmale = nothing, sexratio = nothing)

Mating with controlled family sizes. In every generation the N_g offspring are split among the dams, and every dam is mated to one sire, so all her offspring are full sibs; the dams are split among the sires. A sire's family is the sum of the families of his dams. Sex is assigned to the parents as by RandomMating with the same nmale and sexratio.

dams sets how the offspring are split among the dams, and sires how the dams are split among the sires:

  • :equal: as equal as possible; when the split is not exact, the parents that receive one more are drawn at random.
  • :random: a uniform multinomial split, as each offspring choosing its dam (or each dam its sire) at random. Family sizes are then binomial, close to Poisson.
  • a positive number k: a multinomial split with weights drawn from Gamma(k, 1) for every parent in every generation (Dirichlet-multinomial counts, close to negative binomial). Smaller k means more variable families; as k → ∞ it approaches :random.

With n_d offspring per dam among N_f dams, two offspring share their dam with probability P_f = E[Σ n_d(n_d-1)] / (N(N-1)); they share their sire when they share their dam, or else when their dams share a sire. Two genes in different offspring then come from the same parent with probability a = (P_f + P_m)/4, and the effective size of the generation is about 1/a + 1/2 (1/a = N for N/2 males and N/2 females under random mating), which Hill's (1972) 8N/(V_m + V_f + 4) approximates.

nsires and ndams restrict breeding to a subset of the parental cohort: in every generation, nsires of the males and ndams of the females, each a uniformly random subset, are the sires and dams, and the other individuals have no offspring. The splits are then among these breeders, and the coalescence probabilities above use nsires and ndams in place of the numbers of males and females. With binomial sex, the sexes are redrawn until there are at least nsires males and ndams females (exact rejection sampling, as for the presence of both sexes); with nmale or sexratio, every parental cohort must hold them, which is checked before the run. Hierarchical is the nested design with equal splits.

Selection. With a fitness model (SiteFitness, TraitFitness), the weight of every parent in a split is multiplied by its fitness. With :random splits each dam's expected number of offspring, and each sire's expected number of dams, is proportional to its fitness within its sex, and so is each sire's expected number of offspring; with a Gamma shape the weights are Gamma(k) × fitness. :equal splits, and therefore Hierarchical, cannot be combined with selection, as equal families leave nothing to select on. Breeder subsets (nsires, ndams) are drawn at random, and selection acts among the breeders.

The census size is not affected. effective_size returns the effective size realized by the family sizes of a run, 1/a per generation.

Examples

using FisherWright

# Equal dam families, 5 sires with equal numbers of dams
haps, _ = fisher_wright(50, 20, [100_000], 1.0; mating = FamilySize(dams = :equal, sires = :equal, nmale = 5))

# Highly variable dam families
haps, _ = fisher_wright(50, 20, [100_000], 1.0; mating = FamilySize(dams = 0.5))
source
FisherWright.Hierarchical — Function
Hierarchical(sires, dams_per_sire; nmale = nothing, sexratio = nothing)

Nested mating design: in every generation sires males and sires * dams_per_sire females, uniformly random subsets of the parental cohort, are the breeders; each sire is mated to dams_per_sire of the dams, and the offspring are split equally among the dams (the dams that receive one more, when the split is not exact, are drawn at random). The other individuals have no offspring.

The same as FamilySize(dams = :equal, sires = :equal, nsires = sires, ndams = sires * dams_per_sire; nmale, sexratio); see FamilySize for sex assignment and the effective size.

Examples

using FisherWright

# 4 sires with 5 dams each out of 100 individuals per generation
haps, _ = fisher_wright(100, 20, [100_000], 1.0; mating = Hierarchical(4, 5))

FisherWright.Monoecious — Type
Monoecious(; selfing = nothing)

Mating in a population of hermaphrodites: every individual can be either parent of an offspring, and an offspring of one parent is selfed. Each offspring draws its first parent uniformly from the cohort, with replacement. Its second parent is drawn by one of two models:

  • Monoecious(): uniformly from the whole cohort, the first parent included, so offspring are selfed at random, with probability 1/N (random union of gametes; the monoecious Wright–Fisher model of msprime's DTWF).
  • Monoecious(selfing = s): the first parent itself with probability s, and otherwise uniformly from the N - 1 other individuals, so exactly a proportion s of the offspring are selfed in expectation, 0 ≤ s ≤ 1. selfing = 0 excludes selfing.

There are no sexes: the males record of a result is 0 for such a generation, and the pedigree lists the first parent as sire, the second as dam and the sex as missing. With a fitness model, both parents are drawn with probability proportional to fitness, the second among the others when the offspring is not selfed.

With a selfing rate s the equilibrium inbreeding coefficient is F = s/(2 - s), and the effective size is N/(1 + F) = N(2 - s)/2 (Pollak 1987; Nordborg and Donnelly 1997); with random selfing it is N. effective_size gives the value realized by a run.

Examples

using FisherWright

# A population of 50 that selfs half of its offspring: Ne ≈ 37.5
res = fisher_wright(50, 100, [1_000_000], 1.0; mating = Monoecious(selfing = 0.5), result = true)
effective_size(res)

FisherWright.AvoidRelatives — Type
AvoidRelatives(inner = RandomMating(); level = :halfsib, max_tries = 100)

Mating as by inner, a RandomMating or Monoecious scheme, without matings between relatives. Each offspring draws its sire (first parent) as inner does; a dam (second parent) related to it is rejected and redrawn, as inner draws it, until an unrelated one is found. Relatives are judged on the pedigree of the run, at one of three nested levels:

  • :fullsib: the two share both parents;
  • :halfsib: they share at least one parent (full and half sibs);
  • :cousin: they share at least one parent or grandparent (sibs and first cousins, full or half).

After max_tries draws of the dam without an unrelated one the last pair is kept: the result counts such offspring in related_matings, generation by generation, so a population too small or too related to avoid relatives shows in the record rather than stopping the run. With Monoecious, whether an offspring is selfed is decided as without avoidance, and only an outcrossed second parent is redrawn, among the others.

The individuals of the starting population have unknown parents, and are unrelated to one another; their offspring have unknown grandparents. A run continued through init starts the pedigree anew in the same way.

Under selection the sire is drawn in proportion to fitness and so is every draw of the dam: the dam of a sire is drawn in proportion to fitness among the females unrelated to him. Rejection on the dam alone keeps the sires' usage as under inner, while females with many relatives among the males have fewer offspring.

Examples

using FisherWright

# No full- or half-sib matings, 10 sires
m = AvoidRelatives(RandomMating(nmale = 10); level = :halfsib)
res = fisher_wright(50, 100, [1_000_000], 1.0; mating = m, result = true)
sum(res.related_matings)   # offspring of relatives that could not be avoided

FisherWright.CustomMating — Type
CustomMating(f; sexes = false)

Mating by a user function: in every generation f(ctx) is called with a MatingContext and fills ctx.pm[i, 1] and ctx.pm[i, 2], the two parents of each offspring i = 1:ctx.noffspring, as indices 1:ctx.n into the parental cohort. Its return value is ignored. Write it with do syntax:

assortative = CustomMating() do ctx
    # pair each offspring's parents among neighbours in phenotype
    order = sortperm(ctx.phenotypic)
    for i in 1:ctx.noffspring
        k = rand(ctx.rng, 1:ctx.n - 1)
        ctx.pm[i, 1], ctx.pm[i, 2] = order[k], order[k + 1]
    end
end

With sexes = false (the default) the parents are hermaphrodites, as under Monoecious: any parent can take either role, an offspring of one parent in both is selfed, males is recorded as 0 and the pedigree sex as missing. With sexes = true the callback also sets ctx.sex[k] for every parent k, and every sire must be male and every dam female.

The table is checked after each call, and an index outside 1:ctx.n, or a sire or dam of the wrong sex, stops the run with an ArgumentError. Everything else is the callback's responsibility: the distribution of family sizes, any selection (ctx.fitness holds the fitness of the parents, which the run does not otherwise use), and reproducibility, for which every random draw should come from ctx.rng. The callback runs serially, once per generation; it should not allocate in a long run if speed matters, and should not keep references to the context, whose buffers are reused.

The record of the result (paternal_pairs, maternal_pairs, selfing) and effective_size describe the realized matings as for the built-in schemes.

source
FisherWright.MatingContext — Type
MatingContext

What a CustomMating callback sees of one generation, and where it writes the matings. Parents are numbered 1:n within their cohort.

  • generation::Int: the generation being born, numbered as in the census record of the result (a run continued through init continues it).
  • n::Int: the number of parents; noffspring::Int: the number of offspring.
  • pm: the noffspring × 2 mating table to fill: pm[i, 1] and pm[i, 2] are the sire and dam (first and second parent) of offspring i.
  • sex::Vector{Bool}: the sex of each parent, true for male, to be set by a callback with sexes = true; ignored otherwise.
  • fitness: the relative fitness of each parent under the fitness model of the run, as the built-in schemes use it (only ratios within the cohort matter; 1 for all under Neutral). The callback applies selection itself.
  • ids::UnitRange{Int}: the pedigree IDs of the parents (see first_id of FisherWrightResult).
  • relatives: a 6 × n matrix: rows 1–2 are the parents and rows 3–6 the grandparents of each parent, as indices into the cohorts before (rows 1–2 of a grandparent's children are those of its cohort); 0 is unknown, as for the starting population.
  • haplotypes: the 2n haplotypes of the parents (individual i is 2i - 1 and 2i), neutral mutation positions, read-only.
  • genotypic, phenotypic: the genotypic value and phenotype of each parent under TraitFitness; 0 otherwise.
  • rng: the random number generator of the run, to be used for every draw so that the run stays reproducible.

Census size

FisherWright.bottleneck — Function
bottleneck(n, nb, start, duration; recovery = n) -> Function

Census trajectory g -> N_g for the popsize keyword of fisher_wright: nb individuals are born in each of the duration generations start, …, start + duration - 1, n in every generation before them and recovery in every generation after them.

Generation g draws its parents from the N_{g-1} individuals of the previous generation, so the nb individuals of a bottleneck generation are the parents of the next one: drift is increased in generations start + 1, …, start + duration.

All sizes must exceed 1, and start and duration must be positive. The function can also be broadcast over generations to build or combine trajectories as vectors, for example bottleneck(100, 10, 21, 5).(1:60).

Examples

using FisherWright

# 100 individuals, reduced to 10 in generations 21 to 25, then 100 again
res = fisher_wright(100, 60, [1_000_000], 1.0; popsize = bottleneck(100, 10, 21, 5), result = true)
res.census[20:28]   # N_19, …, N_27: 100, 100, 10, 10, 10, 10, 10, 100, 100

growth

FisherWright.growth — Function
growth(n0, r; start = 0, limit = nothing) -> Function

Census trajectory g -> N_g for the popsize keyword of fisher_wright: geometric growth at rate r per generation from n0 individuals, N_g = round(Int, n0 * (1 + r)^(g - start)) for g > start and n0 up to generation start. r > -1; a negative r is a decline.

With limit, the size stops there: it is the ceiling of a growing population (r > 0) and the floor of a declining one (r < 0). Without it, a decline that reaches a size below 2 is rejected by fisher_wright, which names the generation, and growth is bounded only by memory: the run allocates for the largest size of the trajectory.

n0 should be the starting size ne of the run, so that the first generation continues from it. The function can be broadcast over generations to obtain the trajectory as a vector, growth(50, 0.05).(1:40).

Examples

using FisherWright

# 5% growth per generation from 50 individuals, up to 500
res = fisher_wright(50, 60, [1_000_000], 1.0; popsize = growth(50, 0.05; limit = 500), result = true)
res.census[[1, 2, 11, 61]]   # 50, 52, 81, 500

# Constant for 20 generations, then a 10% decline per generation down to 10
decline = growth(50, -0.1; start = 20, limit = 10)

Selection at sites

FisherWright.SiteFitness — Type
SiteFitness(loci::FitnessLoci)

Multiplicative fitness over the selected sites of loci: an individual's fitness is the product over sites of 1, 1 + h*s or 1 + s for zero, one or two copies of the derived allele. Pass it as the fitness keyword of fisher_wright.

Selection is soft: fitness is evaluated on the adults of each generation, and each offspring draws its sire and its dam with probability proportional to fitness within each sex, with replacement. The census size is not affected, and only fitness relative to the other parents of the same sex matters.

source
FisherWright.FitnessLoci — Type
FitnessLoci(pos, s; h = 0.5, μ = 0.0)

Table of selected sites, fixed for a run.

  • pos: genome-wide positions of the sites, strictly increasing.
  • s: selection coefficient of the derived allele at each site (a number for all sites, or one per site), s ≥ -1.
  • h: dominance at each site (a number or one per site), with 1 + h * s ≥ 0.
  • μ: mutation rate to the derived allele per site per haplotype per generation; 0 means the selected alleles of a run are those of its starting population.

A genotype at one site has fitness 1, 1 + h*s and 1 + s with zero, one and two copies of the derived allele; see SiteFitness for how sites combine. Sites are biallelic and there is no back-mutation: a mutation at a site the haplotype already carries has no effect.

Fields

pos::Vector{UInt32}, s::Vector{Float64}, h::Vector{Float64}, μ::Float64.

See also

fitness_loci, SiteFitness

source
FisherWright.fitness_loci — Function
fitness_loci(chr, nsites; s, h = 0.5, μ = 0.0, rng = Random.default_rng()) -> FitnessLoci

Draw nsites distinct selected sites uniformly over a genome with chromosome lengths chr and build their FitnessLoci table.

  • s: a number, used for every site, or a univariate distribution from Distributions.jl, from which one coefficient per site is drawn (the distribution of fitness effects), for example -Gamma(0.2, 0.05).
  • h: a number, or a function s -> h applied to each coefficient.
  • μ: mutation rate per site per haplotype per generation.

The draws come from rng only, so the table is reproducible and can be kept with the simulated data as the true effects.

Examples

using FisherWright, Distributions, Random

loci = fitness_loci([1_000_000, 1_000_000], 200; s = -Gamma(0.2, 0.05), μ = 1e-5, rng = Xoshiro(1))

Selection on a trait

FisherWright.TraitFitness — Type
TraitFitness(loci::TraitLoci; σe = 0.0, fitness)

Selection on a quantitative trait. Each generation, every adult has the genotypic value G given by loci (the sum over sites, including sites whose derived allele was fixed and moved to the substitutions), the phenotype P = G + e with e ~ Normal(0, σe²) drawn anew for every individual and generation, and the fitness fitness(P). fitness is Stabilizing, Directional, or any function that returns a non-negative number. Pass it as the fitness keyword of fisher_wright.

Selection is soft and acts within each sex, as for SiteFitness: every offspring draws its sire among the males and its dam among the females with probability proportional to fitness, with replacement.

The environmental deviations are drawn serially from the rng of the run, before mating, so a run does not depend on the number of threads.

Examples

using FisherWright, Distributions

chr = [1_000_000, 1_000_000]
loci = trait_loci(chr, 200; a = Normal(0, 0.2), μ = 1e-4)
f = TraitFitness(loci; σe = 1.0, fitness = Stabilizing(0.0, 5.0))
res = fisher_wright(100, 500, chr, 1.0; fitness = f, result = true)
res.mean_genotypic[end], res.var_genotypic[end]
source
FisherWright.TraitLoci — Type
TraitLoci(pos, a; h = 0.5, μ = 0.0)

Table of the loci of a quantitative trait, fixed for a run.

  • pos: genome-wide positions, strictly increasing.
  • a: effect of the derived allele at each site (a number for all sites, or one per site): the genotypic value of the derived homozygote. Any finite value.
  • h: dominance at each site (a number or one per site): the heterozygote has value h * a. With h = 0.5 the site is additive.
  • μ: mutation rate to the derived allele per site per haplotype per generation; 0 means the trait alleles of a run are those of its starting population.

The genotypic value of an individual is the sum over sites of 0, h*a and a for zero, one and two copies of the derived allele, so that an individual without derived alleles has value 0. Sites are biallelic and there is no back-mutation.

Fields

pos::Vector{UInt32}, a::Vector{Float64}, h::Vector{Float64}, μ::Float64.

See also

trait_loci, TraitFitness

Draw nsites distinct trait loci uniformly over a genome with chromosome lengths chr and build their TraitLoci table.

FisherWright.Stabilizing — Type
Stabilizing(θ, ω²)

Gaussian stabilizing selection on the phenotype P: fitness exp(-(P - θ)^2 / (2ω²)), with optimum θ and width ω² > 0. For use in TraitFitness.

source
FisherWright.Directional — Type
Directional(β)

Directional selection on the phenotype P: fitness exp(β P). For a normally distributed phenotype with variance σ²_P, the selection differential is β σ²_P. For use in TraitFitness.
