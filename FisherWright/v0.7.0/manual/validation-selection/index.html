Validation of selection

Selection in fisher_wright (fitness = SiteFitness(loci)) is checked by the fixation probability of one new copy of a selected allele, which depends on every part of the mechanism: the fitness of each genotype, the draw of parents in proportion to fitness, and the transmission of the selected allele through meiosis.

Model

  • $N$ diploid individuals, discrete non-overlapping generations, two sexes. Sex is Binomial($N$, ½) per generation, conditioned on both sexes being present, or exactly $N/2$ males where stated.
  • One selected site; a genotype has fitness $1$, $1 + hs$ or $1 + s$ with zero, one or two copies of the derived allele.
  • Soft selection within each sex: every offspring draws its sire among the males and its dam among the females with probability proportional to fitness, with replacement.
  • A replicate starts from one copy on a random haplotype of a population without other variation, has no further mutation, and runs to loss or fixation of the allele.

Reference

Kimura's diffusion formula and the exact Markov chain of an ideal Wright–Fisher population describe a single pool of $2N$ genes. The two-sex model differs from that by terms of order $1/N$:

  • the binomial sex ratio lowers the variance effective size to about $4N_mN_f/N$, and the probability of fixation of a favoured allele is proportional to $N_e/N$;
  • a rare allele competes only within the sex of its carrier, where its frequency is twice its frequency in the population, so its advantage over the mean of its competitors is reduced by about $s/(2N)$ more than in a single pool.

The reference for the implementation is therefore an independent simulation of the same model, fixation-reference.jl. It follows one locus as genotype counts and shares no code with the package: sex by coin flips, parents by rejection sampling on fitness, one Bernoulli draw per transmitted gene. The ideal Wright–Fisher value (wf_exact, the exact chain for $2N$ genes) is printed beside it.

Recurrent mutation at many sites

The fixation probability checks one new allele at one site. validate-selection-balance.jl checks the full multi-site path of fisher_wright: mutation at the fitness loci, selection on the product of fitness over sites, and the extraction of selected substitutions.

Design. $N = 30$, $K$ selected sites, each on its own chromosome of 100 bp, so that the sites recombine freely and the neutral process is switched off. The population starts without variation and runs for $T = 300$ generations ($10N$); at the end, each site is classified by its derived-allele count, with substituted sites in the class "fixed". The reference is a naive simulation of one site of the same model (the mutation probability per gene and generation, $1 - e^{-\mu}$, follows from the engine's Poisson hits). Sites in one population share its pedigree, so the unit of replication of the engine is the run; standard errors are taken over runs.

The reference omits the other $K - 1$ sites. Selection at unlinked sites adds variance in offspring number and lowers the effective size at each site (Robertson 1961), by a factor of about $1/(1 + 4C^2)$, $C^2$ the variance of relative fitness (Santiago and Caballero 1995). The checked cases keep $C^2$ at 0.002 or less, where the effect is below 1% and below the resolution. Case 5 is case 1 with 50 sites, $C^2 = 0.010$, and is reported but not checked; it is compared with the reference at $N$ and at $N - 1$.

Selection on a trait

validate-trait.jl checks TraitFitness against quantitative-genetic theory. The mechanism itself, that one generation of selection moves the mean genotypic value by exactly the fitness-weighted mean of the parents within each sex, is tested exactly in CI ("Selection on a trait").

Neutral genetic variance. $K = 20{,}000$ additive loci ($h = ½$, so the allele substitution effect is $\alpha = a/2$) with effects $a \sim N(0, 0.2^2)$ drawn anew for every replicate, mutation $\mu = 10^{-5}$ per site ($U = K\mu = 0.2$ per haplotype and generation), constant fitness, a population without variation at the start. For allele indicators, the sample variance of the dosage among the $N$ individuals has expectation $2H_B - H_W$; with independent effects of zero mean the loci add, so

\[E[\operatorname{var} G] = E[\alpha^2]\,(2H_B - H_W),\]

with $H_B$ and $H_W$ from the two-sex recursion with $2U$ new alleles per pair of genes (infinite sites). The expectation is exact at every generation, not only at equilibrium, where it is $2N_eV_m$ with $N_e = 1/a$ and $V_m = 2UE[\alpha^2]$ (Lynch and Hill 1986). With $\sigma_e > 0$, also $E[\operatorname{var} P] = E[\operatorname{var} G] + \sigma_e^2$.

Directional response, for comparison. With fitness $e^{\beta P}$ and Gaussian $G$ and $P$, the fitness-weighted mean of $G$ is shifted by $\beta V_G$ and its variance is unchanged (no Bulmer effect), so the response per generation is about $\beta V_G$. In a finite population the weighted mean over $n$ parents of a sex is a ratio estimator, with a first-order bias of $-\beta V_G e^{\beta^2 V_P}/n$.

CI test

The testset "Selection under recurrent mutation" runs case 1 of the balance check with 1,000 runs against the reference values (about 10 s on one thread); a dominance of $h = 0.1$ instead of ½ is 5.3 standard errors away.

The testset "Fixation probability under selection" runs four of the fixation ($N = 30$; $s = 0.2$ with $h$ = 0, ½ and 1, and $s = 0$) with 4,000 replicates each and compares them with the reference values of the first table. The three values for $s = 0.2$ are far apart, so an error in the fitness of the heterozygote would be caught. Other CI tests check the parts separately: the fitness of an individual against the product over a dense genotype, the joint meiosis of neutral and selected alleles against the recombinant of their union, the mutation rate at the fitness loci, and the weighted draw of parents.

Not covered

  • Stabilizing selection against theory: the equilibrium genetic variance under mutation and stabilizing selection depends on the regime (Gaussian, house of cards) and is not compared; the mechanism is tested exactly, and the mean is held at the optimum in CI.
  • Linked selection: the effect of selected sites on neutral diversity (background selection, sweeps) against SLiM.
  • Large $N$ and weak selection ($4Nhs$ of order 1 at large $N$), where the comparison with diffusion theory (Poisson random field) would be the natural reference; and a distribution of fitness effects rather than one $s$ per run.
  • Strong interference between sites (Hill–Robertson effects, background selection on linked neutral sites).
