Catalogue of Chapters

This guide walks through the worked examples chapter by chapter, highlighting both the quantitative-genetic models in Mrode & Pocrnic (2023) and the way each calculation is set up in Julia using DataFrames and explicit block matrices.

Chapter 4: Best Linear Unbiased Prediction of Breeding Values

Chapter 4 introduces the foundational Mixed Model Equations (MME) for a single trait measured on beef calves (pre-weaning gain, WWG), comparing the full animal model with the sire model, the Reduced Animal Model (RAM), and unknown parent groups (UPG):

  • ex_04_1 (Sections 4.1–4.3): Univariate animal model for pre-weaning gain. The pedigree, sex, and records of all 8 animals are kept in a single DataFrame (ainv(ped) uses only its sire and dam columns, while unrecorded founders have missing records). We write out the MME in two equivalent ways: first in the general form with $R^{-1}$ (which also works when residual variances are heterogeneous and gives $\mathrm{PEV} = C^{-1}$ directly), and then in the textbook's homogeneous-residual form $[X'X\ X'Z;\ Z'X\ Z'Z + \lambda A^{-1}]$ obtained by multiplying through by $\sigma_e^2$ ($\lambda = \sigma_e^2/\sigma_a^2 = 2$). Both are checked against mme. We then compute prediction error variances ($\mathrm{PEV}_i = d_i\sigma_e^2$), reliabilities ($r_i^2 = 1 - d_i\lambda$), standard errors of prediction ($\mathrm{SEP}$), and decompose each animal's EBV into Parent Average ($\mathrm{PA}$), Yield Deviation ($\mathrm{YD}$), and Progeny Contribution ($\mathrm{PC}$), along with the Daughter Yield Deviation ($\mathrm{DYD}$) of sire 3. All animal-level results are added as columns of ped.
  • ex_04_2 (Section 4.4): Sire model on the same data table as ex_04_1. Only sires with recorded progeny (sires 1, 3, and 4) enter the model; their pedigree is extracted from ped (sire 4 is a son of sire 1, while dams are treated as unknown) and recoded 1:3 to build $A_s^{-1}$. Because a sire transmits half of his breeding value, $\sigma_s^2 = \frac{1}{4}\sigma_a^2 = 5$, and the remaining $\frac{3}{4}\sigma_a^2$ moves into the residual ($\sigma_e^2 = 55$, $\alpha_s = 11$). We write out $[X'X\ X'Z_s;\ Z_s'X\ Z_s'Z_s + \alpha_s A_s^{-1}]$, check it against mme, and compare $\hat{s}$ side by side with $\frac{1}{2}\hat{a}$ from the animal model: although $s = \frac{1}{2}a$ in theory, the estimates differ—and even rank the three sires differently—because the sire model ignores sire 4's own phenotype and the merit of the mates.
  • ex_04_3 (Section 4.5): Reduced Animal Model (RAM) on the data table of ex_04_1. Only parents (animals 1–6) are given equations in the MME; for non-parents (calves 7 and 8), their breeding value is written as $\frac{1}{2}(a_s + a_d) + m_i$ and the Mendelian sampling term $m_i$ is absorbed into the residual. Because parent and non-parent records now have different residual variances ($\sigma_e^2$ vs $\sigma_e^2 + m_i\sigma_a^2$), $R \ne I\sigma_e^2$ and there is no single scalar $\lambda$: we write the MME in the general $R^{-1}$ form $[X'R^{-1}X\ X'R^{-1}W;\ W'R^{-1}X\ W'R^{-1}W + A_p^{-1}/\sigma_a^2]$, solve the 8 equations for fixed effects and parents, and back-solve non-parents 7 and 8 explicitly (Eqn 4.26), recovering the exact solutions of the 10-equation animal model.
  • ex_04_4 (Section 4.6): Animal model with unknown parent groups (UPG) on the data table of ex_04_1. Unknown sires and dams are replaced by negative group codes (-1 for group 1, -2 for group 2). We compute the group contribution matrix $Q$, build the Quaas–Pollak (QP) augmented inverse $A^{*-1} = [A^{-1}\ -A^{-1}Q;\ -Q'A^{-1}\ Q'A^{-1}Q]$ from its blocks (and check it against ainv_upg), and write out the $3 \times 3$ block MME for $[\hat{b};\ \hat{a}^*;\ \hat{g}]$. Finally, we fit the explicit group model $y = Xb + ZQg + Za + e$ to verify that the QP solutions satisfy $\hat{a}^* = \hat{a} + Q\hat{g}$, and contrast them with adding $Q\hat{g}$ to $\hat{a}$ from a model that ignored groups.

Chapter 5: Models for Repeated Records

Chapter 5 extends the mixed model to traits measured several times on the same animal (repeatability models) or on littermates sharing a common environment:

  • ex_05_1 (Sections 5.1–5.2): Repeatability animal model for fat yield across two lactations, fitting both additive genetic effects ($a$) and permanent environmental effects ($pe$). Because each cow has two records, we use two tables: ped (one row per animal) and rec (one row per lactation). We write out the $3 \times 3$ block MME with $\lambda_a A^{-1}$ and $\lambda_{pe} I$, check it against mme, and inspect $\mathrm{rank}(X) = 4$ (with 6 columns) to see why two fixed-effect levels must be constrained to zero (HYS 1 occurs only in parity 1 and HYS 3 only in parity 2). Per-cow yield deviations are computed from rec with groupby/combine, joined onto ped, and used to partition the EBVs and compute the Daughter Yield Deviation ($\mathrm{DYD}$) of sire 1.
  • ex_05_2 (Section 5.3): Animal model with common environmental (litter) effects ($c$) for piglet weaning weight. The pedigree, sex, litter, and weaning weight of all 15 pigs live in one DataFrame. We write out the $3 \times 3$ block MME with $\lambda_a A^{-1}$ and $\lambda_c I$, check it against mme, store the estimated litter effects in a litters table, and join them back onto the piglets in ped.

Chapter 6: Multivariate Models

Chapter 6 shows how to evaluate genetically and environmentally correlated traits jointly, from complete balanced data to missing records, unequal designs, sex-limited traits, and international sire evaluations (MACE):

  • ex_06_1 (Sections 6.1–6.2): Bivariate animal model for pre-weaning gain (WWG) and post-weaning gain (PWG) with equal design matrices and no missing records, stored in a single DataFrame. With traits stacked ($y = \mathrm{vec}(Y)$), the MME take the clean Kronecker-product form $[R_0^{-1}\otimes X'X\ R_0^{-1}\otimes X'Z;\ R_0^{-1}\otimes Z'X\ R_0^{-1}\otimes Z'Z + G_0^{-1}\otimes A^{-1}]$, which we write out with kron and check against the general multi-trait assembly (mt_stack, mt_blocks, mt_rinv). Multi-trait and univariate EBVs, prediction error variances, and reliabilities are placed side by side as columns of ped, and calf 8's bivariate evaluation is partitioned into $\hat{a}_8 = W_1\mathrm{PA}_8 + W_2\mathrm{YD}_8$.
  • ex_06_2 (Section 6.3): Bivariate animal model with equal design matrices when calves 4 and 9 are missing PWG. To see how missing records enter the MME, we write out the padded Kronecker form in which missing observations are set to zero and each calf contributes $(R_0[S,S])^{-1}$ on its observed trait subset $S$ (and zeros elsewhere), and check it against mt_stack, mt_blocks, and mt_rinv (which drop missing records instead of padding). Comparing the bivariate and univariate columns of ped shows how calves 4 and 9 gain PWG information from their own WWG records through the genetic correlation.
  • ex_06_3 (Section 6.4): Fat yield in parities 1 and 2 analyzed as two correlated traits with unequal design matrices. We take the long-format lactation table from ex_05_1 and reshape it to one row per cow with unstack. Because HYS 1–2 occur only in parity 1 and HYS 3–4 only in parity 2, each trait has its own design matrix $X_t$, so the MME replace $I_2 \otimes X$ with $X_b = \mathrm{blockdiag}(X_1, X_2)$. We also compute the multi-trait $\mathrm{DYD}$ of sire 1 (Section 6.4.2).
  • ex_06_4 (Section 6.5.1): Multivariate analysis with zero residual covariance: yearling weight recorded only on males and fat yield recorded only on females, in one 17-animal DataFrame. Because no animal has both traits and $R_0 = \mathrm{diag}(\sigma_{e1}^2, \sigma_{e2}^2)$ is diagonal, the fixed-effect and data blocks of the two traits are completely separate; we write out the $4 \times 4$ block MME to show that information flows between males and females solely through the off-diagonal blocks $g^{12} A^{-1}$ of $G_0^{-1} \otimes A^{-1}$.
  • ex_06_5 (Section 6.5.2): Multi-trait Across-Country Evaluation (MACE) of bulls using a sire–maternal-grandsire (sire–MGS) pedigree with 5 phantom parent groups. We keep a table of bulls, a table of groups, and a table of national proofs (deregressed proofs $\mathrm{DRP}$ and effective daughter contributions $\mathrm{EDC}$ in each country). Because each bull's residual precision $R_{ik}^{-1} = \mathrm{EDC}_{ik}/\sigma_{ei}^2$ is different, we write the MME in the general $R^{-1}$ form with explicit stabilization on the group diagonals, check it against mme, and decompose the MACE proofs of bulls 3 and 2 into parent average, national daughter deviation, and progeny contribution (Eqns 6.19–6.21).

Chapter 8: Maternal Effects Models

For traits like calf birth weight or weaning weight, a calf's record is affected both by its own genotype (the direct genetic effect) and by the milking and mothering ability of its dam (maternal genetic and maternal permanent environmental effects):

  • ex_08_1 (Sections 8.1–8.2): Maternal animal model for calf birth weight on a 14-animal DataFrame. Each calf's record is linked to its own direct genetic effect ($u$) by $Z$, to its dam's maternal genetic effect ($m$) by $W$, and to its dam's permanent environment ($pe$) by $S$. Because $u$ and $m$ are correlated across all relatives ($\mathrm{var}([u;\ m]) = G_0 \otimes A$), their $2 \times 2$ block in the MME is coupled by $\alpha \otimes A^{-1} = (\sigma_e^2 G_0^{-1}) \otimes A^{-1}$. We write out the $4 \times 4$ block MME explicitly, check it against mme, and add the direct, maternal, and $pe$ solutions to ped.
  • ex_08_2 (Section 8.3): Maternal Reduced Animal Model (RAM) on the same dataset. Only parents (animals 1–9) have equations in the MME; the direct Mendelian sampling of non-parent calves (10–14) is absorbed into their residuals. We write the reduced MME in the general $R^{-1}$ form, solve for the parents, and then back-solve the non-parents in pedigree order—first their direct Mendelian sampling (Eqn 8.8), and then their maternal Mendelian sampling by regressing on the direct deviation via $g_{12}/g_{11}$ (Eqn 8.10)—recovering the exact solutions of the full model in ex_08_1.

Chapter 9: Social Interaction Models

When animals are housed in groups (such as pigs in a pen), an individual's growth rate depends not only on its own direct breeding value ($\mathrm{DBV}$, $u_D$) but also on the associative (social) breeding values ($\mathrm{SBV}$, $u_S$) of its pen-mates:

  • ex_09_1 (Sections 9.2–9.4): Social interaction model for 15 pigs (9 recorded in 3 pens of 3) in one DataFrame. We construct the associative incidence matrix directly from the pen incidence matrix $V$ as $Z_S = (VV' - I)Z_D$ (VV' finds records in the same pen, and subtracting $I$ removes the pig itself), and write out the MME (Eqn 9.6) in the compact form $T'T + \Sigma$ with $T = [X\ Z_D\ Z_S\ V\ W]$ (fixed sex, correlated direct and social genetic effects, random pen $g$, and random litter $c$). We then fit the equivalent correlated-residual model (Eqn 9.7, Section 9.4), which omits the explicit pen effect $g$ and instead uses the block-diagonal residual covariance $R = \sigma_e^2[(1-\rho)I + \rho VV']$, verifying that all fixed, genetic, and litter solutions are identical. Finally, we partition pig 7's $(\hat{u}_D, \hat{u}_S)$ into parent average and direct/social yield deviations (Section 9.3), and compare total breeding values $\mathrm{TBV}_i = u_{D,i} + (n-1)u_{S,i}$ with a standard animal model that ignores social effects.

Chapter 10: Random Regression & Covariance Functions

Chapter 10 models longitudinal traits (such as test-day milk or fat yields recorded along a lactation trajectory) using orthogonal Legendre polynomials and linear splines:

  • ex_10_1 (Sections 10.1–10.2): Fixed regression test-day model. Table 10.1 is entered just as printed in the book (one column per cow across 10 Days in Milk, DIM) and reshaped with stack to a long-format table rec with one row per test day. All cows share a single fixed lactation curve of order-4 Legendre polynomials ($X_2$), while each cow has a single constant daily breeding value ($a_j$) and permanent environmental effect ($pe_j$). We write the MME as $T'T + \Sigma$ with $T = [X_1\ X_2\ Z\ Q]$, check it against mme, evaluate the fitted lactation curve, and partition the EBVs by joining per-cow yield deviations onto ped.
  • ex_10_2 (Section 10.3): Random regression test-day model on the same records. Each animal now has its own order-2 Legendre genetic trajectory ($u_{j0}, u_{j1}, u_{j2}$) and permanent environmental trajectory ($pe_{j0}, pe_{j1}, pe_{j2}$). We build the random-regression design matrix $Z_{RR} = [\mathrm{Diag}(\phi_0)Z\ \mathrm{Diag}(\phi_1)Z\ \mathrm{Diag}(\phi_2)Z]$, write the MME as $T'T + \Sigma$ with $\sigma_e^2 G^{-1} \otimes A^{-1}$ and $\sigma_e^2 P^{-1} \otimes I$, and check it against mme. Summing the polynomial covariables from day 6 to day 310 gives the weight vector $t$ used to compute 305-day breeding values ($\hat{u}t$), their reliabilities $1 - (t'C^{ii}t)/(t'Gt)$ (Section 10.3.4), and the multi-trait partition of cow 6's random regression coefficients (Section 10.3.2).
  • ex_10_3 (Sections 10.4 and 10.3.5): Covariance functions and linear splines. Given a $3 \times 3$ genetic covariance matrix $G$ at 90, 160, and 240 days of age, the full-order Legendre covariance function is simply $\hat{C} = \Phi^{-1}G(\Phi')^{-1}$, written in Julia as Φ \ G / Φ'. For the reduced-order ($k = 2$) Weighted Least Squares (WLS) fit (Section 10.4.1), we write out the vectorized design matrix $X = L(\Phi_2 \otimes \Phi_2)D$ (where $L$ extracts the lower triangle $\mathrm{vech}(G)$ and $D$ is the duplication matrix of $C$), solve $(X'V^{-1}X)^{-1}X'V^{-1}\mathrm{vech}(G)$, and check both fits and the $\chi^2$ test against covariance_function. We then tabulate the continuous variance and covariance curves across ages 90–240 and compute the linear spline covariables of Section 10.3.5.

Chapter 11: Genomic Selection & The Bayesian Alphabet

Chapter 11 develops genomic prediction from marker regressions and SNP-BLUP to GBLUP, ancestral genotype imputation, residual polygenic effects, haplotype models, the Bayesian alphabet, and multi-trait GBLUP:

  • ex_11_1 (Sections 11.2–11.3): Fitting the first 3 SNPs as fixed regressions alongside a random polygenic effect ($u \sim N(0, A\sigma_a^2)$), both unweighted and weighted by each bull's Effective Daughter Contribution ($\mathrm{EDC}$). Both analyses share the same general-form MME $[X'R^{-1}X\ X'R^{-1}W;\ W'R^{-1}X\ W'R^{-1}W + A^{-1}/\sigma_a^2]$, with $R^{-1} = I/\sigma_e^2$ or $\mathrm{diag}(\mathrm{EDC}/\sigma_e^2)$. Direct genomic values ($\mathrm{DGV} = Z_3\hat{g}$), polygenic effects ($\hat{u}$), and $\mathrm{GEBV} = \mathrm{DGV} + \hat{u}$ are added as columns of the bulls table.
  • ex_11_2 (Section 11.4.1): SNP-BLUP (ridge regression BLUP) fitting all 10 SNPs as random effects with common variance $\sigma_g^2 = \sigma_a^2 / 2\sum p_j(1-p_j)$, unweighted and EDC-weighted. We write out the general-form MME, check it against mme, and collect the marker solutions in a snps table and the bull predictions in bulls. Notice that SNPs 7 and 8 are monomorphic in the 8 reference bulls: their centred columns are constant (collinear with the overall mean), so ridge regression shrinks their estimated effects to exactly zero.
  • ex_11_3 (Sections 11.4.2–11.4.5 and 11.9): Genomic BLUP (GBLUP) and equivalent formulations. We build VanRaden's first genomic relationship matrix $G = ZZ' / 2\sum p_j(1-p_j)$ (and check it against grm), note that $G$ ($14 \times 14$ from 10 SNPs) has rank at most 10 so $G + 0.01I$ is inverted, and write out the GBLUP MME $[X'X\ X'W;\ W'X\ W'W + \lambda G^{-1}]$. We then show three key results:
    1. GBLUP animal solutions $\hat{a}$ agree with $Z\hat{g}$ from SNP-BLUP up to the $0.01I$ regularization of $G$;
    2. Individual SNP effects can be back-solved directly from GBLUP as $\hat{g} = \frac{1}{k} Z'G^{-1}\hat{a}$ (Example 11.4, checked against snp_from_gblup);
    3. The selection-index form $\hat{a} = G_{\cdot,r}(G_{rr} + \lambda I)^{-1}(y_r - \mathbf{1}\hat{\mu})$ (Example 11.5, checked against gblup_index) needs no $14 \times 14$ inverse of $G$ and reproduces the SNP-BLUP DGVs and theoretical reliabilities.
  • ex_11_6 (Section 11.4.6): Base-population allele frequencies and ancestral gene-content imputation by the linear method of Gengler et al. (2007). Treating each SNP's gene content ($0, 1, 2$) as a highly heritable trait ($\lambda = 0.01$) on the pedigree gives the same left-hand side $[X'X\ X'W;\ W'X\ W'W + \lambda A^{-1}]$ for every marker, so we solve for all 10 SNPs at once using a matrix right-hand side $[X'M;\ W'M]$ and compare with base_allele_frequencies and the GLS estimator.
  • ex_11_7 (Section 11.5): Genomic evaluation with a residual polygenic effect ($u \sim N(0, A\sigma_u^2)$ accounting for $10\%$ of $\sigma_a^2$). We write both the SNP-BLUP + polygenic model (Eqn 11.14, $T = [X\ W\ Z_r]$) and the GBLUP + polygenic model (Eqn 11.16, $T = [X\ W\ W]$) in the compact form $T'T + \Sigma$, check them against mme, and compare their polygenic, genomic, and total breeding values in the bulls table.
  • ex_11_8 (Section 11.6): Haplotype-based GBLUP. Phased haplotypes across 9 SNPs are divided into 3 blocks of 3 SNPs; we write out the pseudo-SNP dosage coding (counting how many strands of each animal carry each distinct 3-SNP haplotype allele in a block) and check it against pseudo_snps. From the centred pseudo-SNPs we form $G_h = Z_h Z_h' / 2\sum pq$, write out the GBLUP MME $[n\ \mathbf{1}';\ \mathbf{1}\ I + \lambda K^{-1}]$, and compare the SNP-based and haplotype-based DGVs (both with the book's misprinted row for animal 5 and with the corrected haplotype coding).
  • ex_11_9 (Section 11.7): The Bayesian Alphabet (BayesA, BayesB, BayesC, and BayesC$\pi$) fitted by single-site Gibbs sampling with residual updating. We verify the deterministic starting values and residuals, run MCMC chains for all four priors, and collect the posterior mean SNP effects and inclusion probabilities alongside the book's values in a snps table and the candidate DGVs in bulls. We also highlight what happens to SNP 7, which is monomorphic ($z_7'z_7 = 0$) in the reference bulls: because the likelihood carries no information about $g_7$, its inclusion indicator is drawn from the prior with probability $1 - \pi$.
  • ex_11_13 (Section 11.8): Multivariate GBLUP for WWG and PWG of the 5 recorded calves from Example 6.1. Because every genotyped calf has its own record ($Z = I$), the MME are the Kronecker equations of ex_06_1 with $Z = I$ and $G^{-1}$ in place of $A^{-1}$. The resulting bivariate DGVs are compared side by side with the pedigree-based EBVs from ex_06_1 in a calves table.

Chapter 12: Single-Step GBLUP and APY

Chapter 12 combines genotyped and ungenotyped animals in a single evaluation (ssGBLUP) and shows how to invert $G$ in linear time for large genotyped populations (APY):

  • ex_12_1 (Section 12.1): Single-step GBLUP (ssGBLUP). We compute $A_{22}$ for the 14 genotyped bulls by Colleau's algorithm, blend $G_b = 0.95G + 0.05A_{22}$, and solve the $2 \times 2$ linear system that matches the mean diagonal and off-diagonal elements of $G_t = a + bG_b$ to $A_{22}$ (checked against tune_grm). We then build the single-step inverse

    \[H^{-1} = A^{-1} + \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 0 \\ 0 & G_t^{-1} - A_{22}^{-1} \end{bmatrix}\]

    explicitly by adding $G_t^{-1} - A_{22}^{-1}$ to the genotyped block of $A^{-1}$ (checked against hinv), solve the ssGBLUP MME $[X'X\ X'W;\ W'X\ W'W + \lambda H^{-1}]$, and compare the ssGBLUP solutions with pedigree BLUP across all 26 animals in ped—showing how paternal half-sib candidates 22–26, which receive identical pedigree BLUPs ($0.030$), are differentiated by their marker genotypes in ssGBLUP.
  • ex_12_2 (Section 12.4): Algorithm for Proven and Young (APY) sparse inversion of $G$, using bulls 15, 20, 22, and 26 as the core subset. We write out the conditional variance of each non-core bull given the core bulls, $m_{ii} = g_{ii} - g_{ic}G_{cc}^{-1}g_{ci}$, assemble $G_{\mathrm{APY}}^{-1}$ from its core and non-core blocks (checking it against apy_ginv), and compare the resulting GBLUP DGVs with those from the full inverse in the bulls table.

Chapter 13: Non-Additive Genetic Models

Chapter 13 extends the linear mixed model to capture dominance deviations (within-locus interactions) and epistasis (between-locus interactions), using both pedigrees and genome-wide markers:

  • ex_13_1 (Sections 13.2–13.3.1): Pedigree-based additive and dominance model for 12 pigs in one DataFrame. We write out Cockerham's rule for the dominance relationship matrix, $D_{ij} = \frac{1}{4}(A_{s_i s_j}A_{d_i d_j} + A_{s_i d_j}A_{d_i s_j})$, check it against drm, and form the MME as $T'T + \Sigma$ with $T = [X\ Z\ Z]$ (since the same incidence matrix $Z$ links a pig's record to its additive value $a$ and dominance deviation $d$).
  • ex_13_2 (Section 13.3.2): Solving directly for total genetic merit $g = a + d$ on the same data. Instead of two random effects per animal, we fit a single genetic vector $g$ with covariance $V = A\sigma_a^2 + D\sigma_d^2$, solve the smaller MME $[X'X\ X'Z;\ Z'X\ Z'Z + \sigma_e^2 V^{-1}]$, and recover the additive and dominance components afterwards as $\hat{a} = \sigma_a^2 A V^{-1} \hat{g}$ and $\hat{d} = \sigma_d^2 D V^{-1}\hat{g}$—confirming that they match the separate model of ex_13_1 to machine precision.
  • ex_13_3 (Section 13.4): Genomic additive and dominance models (Example 13.3) and genomic inbreeding depression (Example 13.4) for 15 genotyped pigs. We write out both the centred additive coding $Z_a = M - 2p'$ and the orthogonal dominance coding $H$ of Vitezica et al. (2013) ($-2q^2, 2pq, -2p^2$ for genotypes $2, 1, 0$), verify $G = Z_a Z_a'/2\sum pq$ and $D = HH'/\sum(2pq)^2$ against grm, and fit the MME $T'T + \Sigma$ ($T = [X\ Z\ Z]$) without and with the proportion of homozygous loci $f_i$ as a fixed covariate for inbreeding depression.
  • ex_13_5 (Section 13.5): Genomic model adding additive $\times$ additive epistasis ($aa_g$). We write out the epistatic relationship matrix $G_{AA}$ as the elementwise (Hadamard) product G .* G scaled by its mean diagonal (checked against epistatic_grm), assemble the MME $T'T + \Sigma$ with $T = [X\ Z\ Z\ Z]$, and collect the additive, dominance, epistatic, and total genetic values in the pigs table.

Chapter 14: Crossbreeding & Breed of Origin of Alleles

Chapter 14 addresses multibreed populations and terminal crossbreeding programs where alleles trace back to distinct founder breeds with different genetic variances:

  • ex_14_1 (Sections 14.2–14.4): Multibreed animal models for 11 animals from two breeds and their crosses, kept in an animals table. We compute breed fractions ($f_1, f_2$) and breed segregation coefficients ($h_{12}$), build the partial relationship matrices $A_1, A_2, A_{12}$ (García-Cortés & Toro, 2006), and write out the MME for three models as $T'T + \Sigma$:
    1. the breed-specific model fitting $a^{(1)}, a^{(2)}, a^{(12)}$ separately;
    2. the equivalent combined model fitting total merit $a = a^{(1)} + a^{(2)} + a^{(12)}$ with $G = \sigma_1^2 A_1 + \sigma_2^2 A_2 + \sigma_{12}^2 A_{12}$;
    3. the random-regression approximation of Strandén & Mäntysaari (2013) (Example 14.2), which uses the single full-pedigree matrix $A$ weighted by $\sqrt{f_1}, \sqrt{f_2}, \sqrt{h_{12}}$.
  • ex_14_3 (Section 14.7): Breed of Origin of Alleles (BOA) models for purebred and crossbred performance (Christensen et al., 2014), first using pedigree partial relationships $A^{(1)}, A^{(2)}$ (Example 14.3) and then using breed-specific genomic matrices $G^{(1)}, G^{(2)}$ built from phased crossbred alleles (Example 14.4). Because crossbred records have a different residual variance ($\sigma_x^2 = 4.75$) from purebred records ($\sigma_e^2 = 4.0$), the MME are written out in the general $R^{-1}$ form with a $2 \times 2$ purebred–crossbred genetic block $S_p^{-1} \otimes K_p^{-1}$ for each breed.

Chapter 15: Non-Linear & Threshold Models

Categorical traits (such as calving ease scored in ordered classes) violate the normality and linearity assumptions of standard BLUP and are modeled via an underlying continuous liability:

  • ex_15_1 (Sections 15.1–15.2): Threshold (probit) sire model for calving ease in 3 ordered categories (Gianola & Foulley, 1983). The data are entered as a table of 20 herd–sex–sire subclasses with category counts (n1, n2, n3). After estimating the thresholds ($t_1, t_2$), fixed effects, and sire transmitting abilities on the liability scale by Fisher scoring (threshold_model), we write out the category probabilities $P(Y = k \mid \eta) = \Phi(t_k - \eta) - \Phi(t_{k-1} - \eta)$ (checked against category_probabilities). For the linear-model comparison, we expand the subclass counts to one row per calving using stack and collect all sire solutions and probabilities in a sires table.
  • ex_15_2 (Section 15.3): Joint analysis of a continuous trait (birth weight, BW) and a binary threshold trait (calving difficulty, CD) across 47 calvings (Foulley et al., 1983), stored in a table of calvings. Conditioning the liability of CD on observed BW introduces the residual regression $\beta = \sigma_{e_{12}} / \sigma_{e_1}^2$. We compare the joint threshold-linear estimates and sire-specific difficult-calving probabilities with the bivariate linear approximation, whose MME are written out in the Kronecker form of ex_06_1 and checked against mme.

Chapter 17: Variance Component Estimation

Before breeding values can be predicted, the genetic and residual variance components must be estimated from the data:

  • ex_17_1 (Sections 17.3 and 17.5): Variance components of a sire model by Henderson's Method 3 and by fitting canonical sums of squares. We write out Method 3 directly from the quadratic-form reductions $R(b) = y'X(X'X)^-X'y$ and $R(b, s)$ and the expected sire coefficient $\mathrm{tr}(Z'S_X Z)$ (checked against henderson3). We then form the orthogonal sire contrasts, fit their mean squares by Ordinary Least Squares (C \ ms) and by a 3-line iterated Generalized Least Squares (GLS) loop (checked against fit_mean_squares), and compare all three sets of estimates in a table.
  • ex_17_2 (Section 17.7): Restricted Maximum Likelihood (REML) for the univariate animal model by Average Information (AI-REML) and Expectation–Maximization (EM-REML), on a single 8-animal DataFrame. Using the MME at the starting values, we write out both the restricted log-likelihood $\log L_R = -\frac{1}{2}(\log|C| + \log|A| + N\log\sigma_e^2 + q\log\sigma_a^2 + y'Py)$ and the first EM-REML update $\sigma_a^{2(1)} = (\hat{a}'A^{-1}\hat{a} + \mathrm{tr}(A^{-1}C^{aa}) \sigma_e^2)/q$ (Eqns 17.5–17.6), check them against reml, and add the EBVs at the converged AI-REML estimates to ped.

Chapter 18: Bayesian Analysis & Gibbs Sampling

Chapter 18 introduces Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) inference for location parameters and variance components in univariate and multivariate animal models:

  • ex_18_1 (Section 18.2): Univariate animal model Gibbs sampler on the data table of ex_04_1. We write out the first Gibbs sweep as an explicit loop of single-site normal updates, $\theta_i \sim N((r_i - \sum_{j \ne i} C_{ij}\theta_j)/C_{ii},\; \sigma_e^2/C_{ii})$, replaying the textbook's 10 normal deviates to reproduce Round 1 exactly and checking it against gibbs_sweep!. We then run full chains—first holding variances fixed to confirm that the posterior means converge to the BLUP solutions, and then sampling $(\sigma_e^2, \sigma_a^2)$ under proper scaled inverse-$\chi^2$ priors—comparing all three sets of animal estimates in ped.
  • ex_18_2 (Section 18.3): Multivariate block Gibbs sampler with inverse-Wishart updates of $R_0$ and $G_0$ on the bivariate data table of ex_06_1. We verify that the posterior means with fixed (co)variances match multi-trait BLUP, demonstrate how a missing PWG record on calf 4 is imputed at each sweep by drawing from its conditional normal distribution given WWG, and tabulate the prior and posterior means of $R_0$ and $G_0$ when (co)variances are sampled.

Chapter 19: Iterative Solvers for Large-Scale Systems

When an evaluation involves millions of animals, inverting or even storing the MME coefficient matrix $C$ is impractical. Chapter 19 demonstrates stationary iterative methods, Iteration on Data (IOD), and Preconditioned Conjugate Gradients (PCG):

  • ex_19_1 (Section 19.4): Jacobi (Example 19.1, with relaxation $\omega = 0.8$ on animal equations) and Gauss–Seidel (Example 19.2) iteration on the explicit MME of ex_04_1. Mrode's starting values (sex means for fixed effects, deviations from the sex mean for recorded calves, and 0 for unrecorded ancestors) are computed from the data table with groupby. Both iterative schemes are written out as explicit loops—showing the difference between using the previous round's vector $x^{(k)}$ in Jacobi versus immediately using updated entries in Gauss–Seidel—checked against jacobi and gauss_seidel, and their convergence criteria are tabulated side by side across all 20 rounds.
  • ex_19_3 (Sections 19.5.1, 19.5.2, and 19.6): Iteration on Data (IOD) for the animal model without assembling $C$:
    • Gauss–Seidel on data (AnimalModelIOD) without unknown parent groups (Example 19.3) and with unknown parent groups 9 and 10 (Example 19.4, both unconstrained and with group 9 fixed to 0 to recover the QP solution of ex_04_4);
    • Preconditioned Conjugate Gradient (PCG) on data (Example 19.6) with diagonal preconditioner $M = \mathrm{diag}(C)$, written out as an explicit loop over the matrix-free product $q = Cp$ (iod(q, p)) and checked against pcg. The solutions of Gauss–Seidel IOD, PCG, and the direct solution of the MME are compared in a table.
  • ex_19_5 (Section 19.5.3): Maternal Reduced Animal Model (the system of ex_08_2) solved by Gauss–Seidel iteration without constraining a fixed-effect level. Because the herd and pen equations are linearly dependent, the coefficient matrix is singular and Gauss–Seidel converges to one particular generalized-inverse solution. Comparing this unconstrained solution with the constrained solution of ex_08_2 ($\mathrm{herd}_1 = 0$) illustrates a fundamental property of linear models: the individual herd and pen numbers differ, but the estimable cell means $\mathrm{herd}_i + \mathrm{pen}_j$ and all random genetic and permanent environmental predictions agree.
