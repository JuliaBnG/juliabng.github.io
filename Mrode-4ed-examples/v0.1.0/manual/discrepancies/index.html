Textbook Discrepancies & Errata

When working through a textbook with a calculator or writing your own code, few things are more frustrating than getting a slightly different number and not knowing whether the mistake is in your algebra or in a printed table.

During the implementation and verification of the worked examples in Mrode & Pocrnic (2023), several typographical slips, sign omissions, and intermediate-rounding differences were found in the 4th edition text. In each case, the example script explains the cause of the difference in a comment and checks the mathematically verified value. All of these notes are gathered below by chapter for easy reference.

Chapter 6: Multivariate Models

  • Example 6.3 (ex_06_3) — Daughter Yield Deviation for Sire 1: In Section 6.4.2, the book computes $\mathrm{DYD}$ for sire 1 using a first-parity yield deviation for daughter 4 of $\mathrm{YD}_{4,1} = 25.7$. However, from the printed record $y_{4,1} = 201$ and HYS solution $\hat{b}_1 = 175.7$, the subtraction is $201 - 175.7 = 25.3$. Correcting that arithmetic slip gives $\mathrm{DYD}_1 = [24.254, 32.154]'$ (instead of the printed $[24.521, 32.154]'$) and $M_3\mathrm{DYD}_1 = [7.388, 7.358]'$ (instead of $[7.439, 7.387]'$).
  • Example 6.4 (ex_06_4) — Zero Residual Covariance: In the printed solutions table, animal 6 is listed as $(-5.012, -2.098)$, an exact duplicate of animal 5. In the pedigree, however, dam 6 is the mother of male 11, who has a high yearling weight record ($300\text{ kg}$ in HYS 2 vs $250\text{ kg}$ for dam 5's son, animal 10). Solving the MME gives $+5.012$ and $+2.098$ for dam
  • Example 6.5 (ex_06_5) — Multi-Trait Across-Country Evaluation (MACE):
    1. For bull 3, who has a record only in country 1, the weight matrix $W_1$ is printed with a non-zero $(1,2)$ entry of $-0.3614$. Because bull 3 has no progeny, $W_1 + W_2 = I_2$, and since column 2 of $W_2 = [0.5771\ 0;\ 0.3614\ 0]$ is zero, the $(1,2)$ entry of $W_1$ is $0$.
    2. Equation (6.21) for predicting a bull's proof in a country where he has no daughters is printed with a minus sign; because $g^{21}/g^{22} = -g_{12}/g_{11}$, the regression term requires a plus sign, which also reproduces the book's own value of $5.001$ for bull 3 in country 2.
    3. For bull 2, the text computes the progeny contribution through his maternal grandson (bull 3) as $4\hat{a}_3 - 2\hat{a}_7$, omitting the maternal granddam group ($\mathrm{G5}$) of bull 3; the exact progeny contribution is $4\hat{a}_3 - 2\hat{a}_7 - \hat{g}_5$.

Chapter 10: Random Regression Models

  • Example 10.1 (ex_10_1) — Legendre Fixed Lactation Curve: From Days in Milk (DIM) 174 onward, the values printed for the fixed lactation curve ($11.0407, 10.9156, \dots$) do not equal $\Phi\hat{b}_2$ evaluated from the book's own fixed-regression solutions $\hat{b}_2$ and Legendre matrix $\Phi$ (at DIM 174, $\Phi_{174}\hat{b}_2 = 11.0994$).
  • Example 10.2 (ex_10_2) — 305-Day Weights and Trajectory Variance:
    1. The cumulative 305-day polynomial weight vector $t = \sum_{d=6}^{310} \phi(d)$ in the book sums Legendre polynomials computed from 4-decimal rounded coefficients, giving $t_3 = -1.5561$ instead of $-1.5451$ at full precision.
    2. In Section 10.3.4, the permanent environmental variance on a 305-day basis, $k'Pk$ (with $k = t$), is printed as $323462.969$, whereas multiplying the book's own $t$ and $P$ gives $320126$.
  • Section 10.3.5 (ex_10_3) — Linear Spline Covariables: In the table of linear spline covariables with knots at days 4, 106, 208, and 310, the printed weights between two knots place the larger weight on the farther knot; ex_10_3 computes standard linear interpolation (where a test day closer to knot $k$ than to knot $k+1$ receives the larger weight on knot $k$).

Chapter 11: Genomic Selection & Bayesian Methods

  • Example 11.1 (ex_11_1) — Candidate IDs: In the table of results, the 6 selection candidates are labelled 25–30 rather than 21–26 (in the 26-animal pedigree of Table 11.1, ancestors are 1–12, reference bulls are 13–20, and candidates are 21–26).
  • Example 11.6 (ex_11_6) — GLS Base Allele Frequency: For SNP 1, the text reports a Generalized Least Squares (GLS) base mean gene content $\hat{\mu} = 2\hat{p}$ of $0.575$. Evaluating $(\mathbf{1}'K\mathbf{1})^{-1}\mathbf{1}'K M_{:,1}$ gives $0.590$ with $K = A_{22}^{-1}$ (close to Gengler's mixed-model estimate of $0.597$) and $0.526$ with the submatrix $K = (A^{-1})_{22}$.
  • Example 11.7 (ex_11_7) — Residual Polygenic Effect: In Table 11.4, the columns headed "DGV" actually tabulate the total breeding value $\mathrm{GEBV} = \mathrm{DGV} + \hat{u}$ (genomic plus residual polygenic effect). In addition, the polygenic component was computed using the 5-generation relationship matrix $A$ printed in Example 11.3 rather than the 2-generation pedigree of Table 11.1.
  • Example 11.8 (ex_11_8) — Haplotype Pseudo-SNPs:
    1. For animal 5 in block 2 (SNPs 4–6), the phased haplotypes are 001 and 000 (alleles 2 and 5 of that block), so the pseudo-SNP row is 01001, but the book prints 01000. The book's $2\sum pq = 4.42$, $G_h$, and haplotype GBLUP solutions follow from that misprinted row; ex_11_8 verifies both the book's table and the corrected haplotype matrix ($2\sum pq = 4.56$).
    2. The text states a correlation of $0.999$ between the SNP-based and haplotype-based $\mathrm{DGV}$s, whereas the correlation computed from the book's own printed solution vectors is $0.992$.
  • Example 11.9 (ex_11_9) — BayesA Starting Residual: At the deterministic starting values ($\mu^{(0)} = 9.888$ and $g_j^{(0)} = 0.05$), the first bull's residual is $9.0 - 9.888 - 9(0.05) = -1.338$, whereas the text prints $-1.388$.
  • Examples 11.10 & 11.12 (ex_11_9) — BayesB Variances & Monomorphic SNP 7: In BayesB (Example 11.10), the book's posterior SNP variances ($\approx 0.30$) are larger than the stationary MCMC posterior mean ($\approx 0.12$ on average across loci, stable over seeds and chain lengths). All other posterior means—including the BayesC$\pi$ posterior $\pi = 0.51$ in Example 11.12—are reproduced. (Note that SNP 7 is $0$ in all 8 reference bulls, so $z_7'z_7 = 0$ and its inclusion indicator is sampled from the prior with probability $1 - \pi$; an earlier discrepancy in BayesC$\pi$ was caused by a $0/0$ log-odds evaluation for monomorphic markers in Breeding prior to v0.1.6.)

Chapter 12: Single-Step GBLUP and APY

  • Example 12.2 (ex_12_2) — APY Inverse Ordering and Sign Slip: The matrix $G_{\mathrm{APY}}^{-1}$ is printed in the textbook with the 4 core bulls (15, 20, 22, 26) ordered first, followed by the 10 non-core bulls, rather than in original bull ID order (13–26). In the solutions table, the minus sign on bull 17's $\mathrm{DGV}$ is omitted ($0.471$ printed instead of $-0.471$).

Chapter 14: Crossbreeding Models

  • Example 14.2 (ex_14_1) — Random-Regression Multibreed Table: Table 14.3 reports the weighted breed contributions $\sqrt{f_p}\,\hat{u}_p$ and $\sqrt{h_{12}}\,\hat{u}_{12}$ (which sum directly to the total breeding value $\hat{a}^*$) rather than the raw random-regression solutions $\hat{u}_p$.
  • Example 14.3 (ex_14_3) — Pedigree Breed of Origin: The solutions match Table 14.5 within $0.005$, but the book's printed values for exchangeable founders (such as animals 1 and 2, which have symmetric roles in the design) show a slight rounding asymmetry ($-0.060$ and $+0.055$ vs exact $\pm 0.057$).
  • Example 14.4 (ex_14_3) — Genomic Breed of Origin: The matrix $G^{(1)}$ printed in the text does not match Equation (14.25) (for instance, its $(1,1)$ entry is printed as $1.815$ instead of $0.607$), but the final BOA solutions in Table 14.9 were computed from the correct $G^{(1)}$ of Equation (14.25) and are reproduced here.

Chapter 15: Non-Linear & Threshold Models

  • Example 15.1 (ex_15_1) — Threshold Sire Model: In Table 15.3, the linear-model comparison column cannot be reproduced from the category counts in Table 15.2. In the table of category probabilities by sire, sire 4's probabilities differ by $0.003$.

Chapter 17: Variance Component Estimation

  • Section 17.5 (ex_17_1) — Canonical Sums of Squares (OLS & SEs): Fitting the three canonical mean squares ($0.3025, 0.1250, 0.1800$) on $C = [1\ 1.5;\ 1\ 1.0;\ 1\ 0.0]$ by unweighted Ordinary Least Squares gives $\hat{\sigma}_e^2 = 0.151$ and $\hat{\sigma}_s^2 = 0.062$ (whereas the text prints $0.143$ and $0.079$). The iterated GLS estimates ($0.163$ and $0.047$) match the book, while the asymptotic sampling variances $\mathrm{diag}((C'V^{-1}C)^{-1})$ are $(0.051, 0.062)$ with standard errors $(0.226, 0.250)$ (compared with $0.216$ and $0.234$ in the text).
  • Section 17.7 (ex_17_2) — REML Starting Solutions: At the starting variance components ($\sigma_e^2 = 0.4, \sigma_a^2 = 0.2$), calf 4's breeding value is printed as $\hat{a}_4 = -0.254$; its sign is actually $+0.254$, which is required to obtain the book's own residual for calf 4 ($2.6 - 2.144 - 0.254 = 0.2022$).

Chapter 18: Bayesian Analysis & Gibbs Sampling

  • Example 18.1 (ex_18_1) — Improper Flat Priors on Variance Components: The textbook specifies flat priors ($\nu_e = \nu_a = -2$, $S^2 = 0$) for the variance components. With only 5 phenotypic records and 8 animals, a flat prior on $\sigma_a^2$ yields an improper joint posterior that diverges at $\sigma_a^2 \to 0$, causing a long Gibbs chain to be absorbed at zero genetic variance. After replaying Round 1 exactly as in the book, ex_18_1 uses weakly informative proper scaled inverse-$\chi^2$ priors ($\nu = 4$) for the full chain.

Chapter 19: Iterative Solvers

  • Example 19.3 (ex_19_3) — Gauss–Seidel Iteration on Data: The column headed "At convergence" in the book is actually round 20; the stated convergence criterion ($< 10^{-7}$) is already satisfied at round 9.
  • Example 19.4 (ex_19_3) — Iteration on Data with Unknown Parent Groups: The column of constrained solutions ($5.474, \dots, -1.795$) printed in the book has not yet converged to the exact constrained solution of Example 4.4 ($5.458, \dots, -1.769$).
  • Example 19.5 (ex_19_5) — Maternal RAM by Gauss–Seidel: The dataset is that of Table 8.1 (Examples 8.1 and 8.2), where the second fixed factor is called "Pen" (levels 1 and 2); in Example 19.5 the book labels those same two levels "Sex" (Male and Female). In addition, because Mrode's stopping rule divides the sum of squared changes by the sum of squared solutions ($\approx 30^2$ for the herd means), stopping at $10^{-14}$ still leaves differences of order $10^{-5}$ in the random effects; ex_19_5 iterates to $10^{-24}$ so that the random effects and estimable cell means match Example 8.2 to 8 decimal places.
