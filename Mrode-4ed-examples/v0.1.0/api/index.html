Mrode4edExamples — Module
Mrode4edExamples

Julia solutions to the worked examples of Mrode and Pocrnic (2023), Linear Models for the Prediction of the Genetic Merit of Animals, 4th edition, built on Breeding.jl and RelationshipMatrices.jl.

Every example is a function ex_CC_N() (chapter CC, script N) that prints its results, compares them with the values printed in the book and returns them. Use list_examples to see the catalogue and run_examples to run (and check) several at once.

Chapter 4: Animal Models & Single-Trait BLUP

Mrode4edExamples.ex_04_1 — Function
ex_04_1() -> NamedTuple

Example 4.1: Univariate animal model for pre-weaning gain (WWG) of beef calves (Mrode & Pocrnic, 2023, Sections 4.1–4.3).

Model Formulation

The standard animal model is:

\[y = Xb + Za + e\]

where:

  • $y$ is the vector of pre-weaning gain records (5 calves: animals 4 to 8).
  • $b$ is the vector of fixed sex effects (Male, Female).
  • $a$ is the vector of random additive genetic effects (breeding values) for all 8 animals in the pedigree, with $\mathrm{var}(a) = A \sigma_a^2$.
  • $e$ is the vector of random residual errors, with $\mathrm{var}(e) = R$.
  • Variance components: $\sigma_a^2 = 20.0$, $\sigma_e^2 = 40.0$.

Key Concepts Illustrated

  1. One table for the data: pedigree, sex and records of all 8 animals in one DataFrame; the founders (1–3) have no records, and their sex is missing as it is not given in the book. ainv(ped) only uses the sire and dam columns, and animal IDs are the row numbers.
  2. MME, general form (any $R$, e.g. heterogeneous residual variances):

    \[\begin{bmatrix} X'R^{-1}X & X'R^{-1}Z \\ Z'R^{-1}X & Z'R^{-1}Z + A^{-1}/\sigma_a^2 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix}\hat{b} \\ \hat{a}\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}X'R^{-1}y \\ Z'R^{-1}y\end{bmatrix}.\]

    Here $\mathrm{PEV} = C^{-1}$ directly.
  3. MME, $R = I\sigma_e^2$: multiplying through by $\sigma_e^2$ gives the form printed in the book, with $\lambda = \sigma_e^2/\sigma_a^2 = 2$:

    \[\begin{bmatrix} X'X & X'Z \\ Z'X & Z'Z + \lambda A^{-1} \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix}\hat{b} \\ \hat{a}\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}X'y \\ Z'y\end{bmatrix}.\]

    This needs a single ratio $\lambda$, i.e. homogeneous residuals. Both forms are written out explicitly and compared with mme.
  4. Accuracy of Evaluations (Section 4.3.3):
    • Prediction error variance: $\mathrm{PEV}_i = d_i \sigma_e^2$, where $d_i$ is the $i$-th diagonal element of the animal block of $C^{-1}$ of the $\lambda$ form.
    • Reliability: $r_i^2 = 1 - \mathrm{PEV}_i / \sigma_a^2 = 1 - d_i \lambda$.
    • Standard error of prediction: $\mathrm{SEP}_i = \sqrt{\mathrm{PEV}_i}$.
  5. Partition of EBV (Sections 4.3.1–4.3.2):
    • Decomposes each animal's EBV into Parent Average (PA), individual Yield Deviation (YD), and Progeny Contribution (PC): $\hat{a}_i = w_1 \mathrm{PA}_i + w_2 \mathrm{YD}_i + w_3 \mathrm{PC}_i$.
    • Daughter Yield Deviation (DYD) of parents (e.g., sire 3).

All animal-level results are added as columns of ped.

Returns

A NamedTuple with fields:

  • ped: The data with columns ebv, d, r2, sep, PA, YD and PC added.
  • b: Fixed effect solutions (sex).
  • a: Vector of estimated breeding values (EBVs) for animals 1 to 8.
  • C: Full inverse of the MME coefficient matrix $C^{-1}$ ($\lambda$ form).
  • partition: Results of EBV decomposition (PA, YD, PC, weights, and DYD).

Example 4.2: Sire model for pre-weaning gain (WWG) of beef calves (Mrode & Pocrnic, 2023, Section 4.4).

Model Formulation

\[y = Xb + Z_s s + e\]

where:

  • $y$ is the vector of pre-weaning gain records for 5 calves.
  • $b$ is the vector of fixed sex effects (Male, Female).
  • $s$ is the vector of random sire transmitting abilities (sires 1, 3, and 4), with $\mathrm{var}(s) = A_s \sigma_s^2$.
  • In a sire model, sire genetic variance is one quarter of additive genetic variance: $\sigma_s^2 = \frac{1}{4} \sigma_a^2 = 5.0$.
  • Residual variance includes the remaining additive genetic variance and environmental variance: $\sigma_e^2 = \sigma_P^2 - \sigma_s^2 = 60.0 - 5.0 = 55.0$.
  • Variance ratio: $\alpha_s = \sigma_e^2 / \sigma_s^2 = 55.0 / 5.0 = 11.0$.

Key Concepts Illustrated

  1. Same data as Example 4.1: one DataFrame with pedigree and records of all 8 animals. The sires with progeny records are taken from it.
  2. Sire Pedigree and Inverse Relationship Matrix: the sire pedigree is derived from ped, in original IDs. Only the sires of sires are kept (sire 4 is a son of sire 1); dams are set to unknown, as in the book. For ainv the sires are recoded 1:3 in order of their IDs, so that parents still precede progeny.
  3. MME, written out explicitly for $R = I\sigma_e^2$:

    \[\begin{bmatrix} X'X & X'Z_s \\ Z_s'X & Z_s'Z_s + \alpha_s A_s^{-1} \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix}\hat{b} \\ \hat{s}\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}X'y \\ Z_s'y\end{bmatrix},\]

    and compared with mme. See ex_04_1 for the general $R^{-1}$ form.
  4. Comparison with the animal model: the transmitting ability is half the breeding value, $s = \frac{1}{2} a$, but the estimates need not agree: the sire model uses only progeny records, and ignores the sires' own records (sire 4), the dams and their relationships. Here even the ranking of the three sires differs from $\frac{1}{2}\hat{a}$ of Example 4.1.

Returns

A NamedTuple with fields:

  • sires: Table of sires with the sire solutions s and $\frac{1}{2}\hat{a}$ of Example 4.1.
  • b: Fixed sex effect solutions.
  • s: Vector of sire transmitting abilities.
  • x: Full MME solution vector.

ex_04_3() -> NamedTuple

Example 4.3: Reduced Animal Model (RAM) for pre-weaning gain of beef calves (Mrode & Pocrnic, 2023, Section 4.5; Quaas & Pollak, 1980).

Model Formulation

The breeding value of a non-parent $i$ is its parent average plus a Mendelian sampling deviation, $a_i = \frac{1}{2}(a_{s_i} + a_{d_i}) + m_i$. Moving the Mendelian sampling of non-parents into the residual gives

\[y = Xb + W a_p + e^*\]

where:

  • Only parents (animals 1 to 6) are directly evaluated in the MME.
  • $W$ maps records to parents: a row has a $1$ for a parent's own record, and $\frac{1}{2}$ for the sire and dam of a non-parent.
  • $\mathrm{var}(a_p) = A_p \sigma_a^2$, with $A_p$ the relationships among parents.
  • Residual variance is $\sigma_e^2$ for records of parents and $\sigma_e^2 + m_i \sigma_a^2$ for records of non-parents, where $m_i = 1 - \frac{1}{4}(1 + F_{s_i}) - \frac{1}{4}(1 + F_{d_i})$ (a term only for each known parent) is the Mendelian-sampling variance factor, $\frac{1}{2}$ here.

Key Concepts Illustrated

  1. Same data as Example 4.1: one DataFrame with pedigree, records and all animal-level results.
  2. Heterogeneous residuals: $R$ is diagonal but not $I\sigma_e^2$, so there is no single variance ratio $\lambda$, and the MME must be written in the general form:

    \[\begin{bmatrix} X'R^{-1}X & X'R^{-1}W \\ W'R^{-1}X & W'R^{-1}W + A_p^{-1}/\sigma_a^2 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix}\hat{b} \\ \hat{a}_p\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}X'R^{-1}y \\ W'R^{-1}y\end{bmatrix}.\]

    It is written out explicitly and compared with mme(...; Rinv).
  3. Back-solving non-parents (Eqn 4.26):

    \[\hat{a}_i = \mathrm{PA}_i + k_i \left( y_i - x_i' \hat{b} - \mathrm{PA}_i \right), \quad \mathrm{PA}_i = \tfrac{1}{2}(\hat{a}_{s_i} + \hat{a}_{d_i}), \quad k_i = \frac{m_i \sigma_a^2}{\sigma_e^2 + m_i \sigma_a^2},\]

    so $k_i = 10/50 = 0.2$ here. Computed explicitly and compared with ram_backsolve.
  4. Same solutions as the full animal model of Example 4.1, from 8 instead of 10 equations.

Returns

A NamedTuple with fields:

  • ped: The data with columns parent, m, rinv, PA, k and ebv added.
  • b: Fixed sex effects solutions.
  • a: Full vector of EBVs for all 8 animals (parents + back-solved non-parents).
  • ap: Parent breeding value solutions from RAM MME.
  • rinv: Diagonal elements of $R^{-1}$.

Mrode4edExamples.ex_04_4 — Function
ex_04_4() -> NamedTuple

Example 4.4: Animal model with unknown parent groups (UPG) using the Quaas–Pollak (QP) transformation (Mrode & Pocrnic, 2023, Section 4.6; Quaas & Pollak, 1981; Westell et al., 1988).

Model Formulation

When ancestors have unknown parents, genetic groups can account for different selection histories or genetic levels among base animals. The explicit model with genetic groups is:

\[y = Xb + ZQg + Za + e\]

where:

  • $g$ is the vector of fixed genetic group effects.
  • $Q$ is the matrix of expected group contributions to individual animals, computed recursively as $Q = (I - P)^{-1} P_Q$.
  • The total genetic merit (EBV including genetic group) is $a^* = Qg + a$.

Quaas–Pollak (QP) Transformation

Instead of adding explicit $ZQg$ columns to the design matrix, the QP transformation defines a modified inverse relationship matrix $A^{*-1}$ (ainv_upg), augmenting the equations directly by genetic groups:

\[A^{*-1} = \begin{bmatrix} I \\ -Q' \end{bmatrix} A^{-1} \begin{bmatrix} I & -Q \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} A^{-1} & -A^{-1}Q \\ -Q'A^{-1} & Q'A^{-1}Q \end{bmatrix}\]

The MME, with $\lambda = \sigma_e^2/\sigma_a^2$, are then

\[\begin{bmatrix} X'X & X'Z & 0 \\ Z'X & Z'Z + \lambda A^{-1} & -\lambda A^{-1}Q \\ 0 & -\lambda Q'A^{-1} & \lambda Q'A^{-1}Q \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix}\hat{b} \\ \hat{a}^* \\ \hat{g}\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}X'y \\ Z'y \\ 0\end{bmatrix},\]

which yield $\hat{g}$ and $\hat{a}^*$ directly without explicit $Q$ multiplications in the design matrix.

Key Concepts Illustrated

  1. Same data as Example 4.1, in one DataFrame, with each unknown parent replaced by its group code: $-1$ for unknown sires (group 1) and $-2$ for unknown dams (group 2). ainv_upg(ped) appends the groups as equations 9 and 10.
  2. $A^{*-1}$ from its blocks: built explicitly from $A^{-1}$ (groups treated as unknown parents) and $Q$, and compared with ainv_upg.
  3. MME, written out explicitly and compared with mme. The sex and group effects are confounded (each row of $Q$ sums to 1), so group 1 is constrained to zero.
  4. Equivalence: $\hat{a}^* = \hat{a} + Q\hat{g}$ from the explicit group model $y = Xb + ZQg + Za + e$.

Returns

A NamedTuple with fields:

  • ped: The data with the group contributions Q1, Q2, and the solutions astar ($\hat{a}^*$), a ($\hat{a}$ of the explicit group model) and a0_Qg (the astar field below) added.
  • groups: Table of the groups with their solutions $\hat{g}$.
  • x: Solution vector from the QP-transformed MME (group 1 constrained to 0).
  • astar: $\hat{a} + Q\hat{g}$ with $\hat{a}$ from the model without groups (Example 4.1), to compare with the QP solutions $\hat{a}^*$.
  • Q: Matrix of genetic group contributions.

Mrode4edExamples.ex_05_1 — Function
ex_05_1() -> NamedTuple

Example 5.1: Repeatability model for dairy cattle fat yield and daughter yield deviation (DYD) (Mrode & Pocrnic, 2023, Sections 5.1–5.2).

Model Formulation

For traits with repeated records per individual (e.g., lactations in dairy cattle):

\[y = Xb + Za + Wpe + e\]

where:

  • $y$ is the vector of 10 lactation fat yield records (cows 4 to 8, parities 1 and 2).
  • $b$ is the vector of fixed herd-year-season (HYS 1 to 4) and parity (1 and 2) effects.
  • $a$ is the vector of random animal additive genetic effects (breeding values), with $\mathrm{var}(a) = A \sigma_a^2$.
  • $pe$ is the vector of permanent environmental effects for cows with records (cows 4 to 8), with $\mathrm{var}(pe) = I \sigma_{pe}^2$.
  • $e$ is the temporary environmental error, with $\mathrm{var}(e) = I \sigma_e^2$.
  • Variance components: $\sigma_a^2 = 20.0$, $\sigma_{pe}^2 = 12.0$, $\sigma_e^2 = 28.0$. Total phenotypic variance $\sigma_P^2 = 60.0$; Repeatability $r = (\sigma_a^2 + \sigma_{pe}^2) / \sigma_P^2 = (20 + 12)/60 = 0.533$.

Key Concepts Illustrated

  1. Two tables: ped with one row per animal, and rec with one row per lactation. Per-cow quantities come from groupby/combine on rec and are joined onto ped; all animal-level results are columns of ped.
  2. Repeatability Model: Fitting both additive genetic and permanent environmental effects. With $R = I\sigma_e^2$ the MME are, with $\lambda_a = \sigma_e^2/\sigma_a^2$ and $\lambda_{pe} = \sigma_e^2/\sigma_{pe}^2$,

    \[\begin{bmatrix} X'X & X'Z & X'W \\ Z'X & Z'Z + \lambda_a A^{-1} & Z'W \\ W'X & W'Z & W'W + \lambda_{pe} I \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix}\hat{b} \\ \hat{a} \\ \hat{pe}\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}X'y \\ Z'y \\ W'y\end{bmatrix},\]

    written out explicitly and compared with mme.
  3. Constraints: $X$ has 6 columns but rank 4. Besides the usual dependency between the HYS and parity levels, HYS 1 occurs only in parity 1 and HYS 3 only in parity 2. Two solutions are therefore set to zero (HYS 1 and 3, as in the book).
  4. Daughter Yield Deviation (DYD) (Section 5.2.3): Records adjusted for fixed effects and permanent environmental effects: $y^* = y - X\hat{b} - W\hat{pe}$. The daughter yield deviation of a sire is the weighted average of adjusted yields of his daughters, corrected for the genetic merit of the daughters' dams.

Returns

A NamedTuple with fields:

  • ped: The pedigree with columns n, YD, ebv, pe, PA, PC and DYD added.
  • rec: The records with the adjusted yields yadj added.
  • b: Fixed effect solutions (HYS and parity).
  • a: Estimated breeding values for all 8 animals in the pedigree.
  • pe: Estimated permanent environmental effects for cows 4 to 8.
  • partition: Partition of sire EBVs and DYD (including DYD for sire 1).

Example 5.2: Animal model with common environmental (full-sib / litter) effects for piglet weaning weight (Mrode & Pocrnic, 2023, Section 5.3).

Model Formulation

\[y = Xb + Za + Wc + e\]

where:

  • $y$ is the vector of weaning weights for 10 piglets (animals 6 to 15).
  • $b$ is the vector of fixed sex effects (Male, Female).
  • $a$ is the vector of random animal additive genetic effects (breeding values) for all 15 animals in the pedigree, with $\mathrm{var}(a) = A \sigma_a^2$.
  • $c$ is the vector of random common environmental effects (litters of dams 2, 4, and 5), with $\mathrm{var}(c) = I \sigma_c^2$.
  • $e$ is the random residual error, with $\mathrm{var}(e) = I \sigma_e^2$.
  • Variance components: $\sigma_a^2 = 20.0$, $\sigma_c^2 = 15.0$, $\sigma_e^2 = 65.0$. Total phenotypic variance $\sigma_P^2 = 100.0$. Heritability $h^2 = 0.20$; common environment ratio $c^2 = \sigma_c^2 / \sigma_P^2 = 0.15$.

Key Concepts Illustrated

  1. One table for the data: pedigree, sex, litter and weaning weight of all 15 animals in one DataFrame; the parents (1–5) have no records. Each dam has one litter here, so litters are named by their dam, but a litter is a level of its own factor: a dam with several litters would have several. The litter solutions are kept in a table of litters and joined back onto the piglets.
  2. Litter / Full-Sib Effects: Distinguishing genetic resemblance from shared maternal/environmental effects.
  3. MME with Multiple Random Effects: an uncorrelated random factor with identity covariance. With $\lambda_a = \sigma_e^2/\sigma_a^2$ and $\lambda_c = \sigma_e^2/\sigma_c^2$,

    \[\begin{bmatrix} X'X & X'Z & X'W \\ Z'X & Z'Z + \lambda_a A^{-1} & Z'W \\ W'X & W'Z & W'W + \lambda_c I \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix}\hat{b} \\ \hat{a} \\ \hat{c}\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}X'y \\ Z'y \\ W'y\end{bmatrix},\]

    written out explicitly and compared with mme.

Returns

A NamedTuple with fields:

  • ped: The data with columns ebv and c (the effect of the piglet's litter) added.
  • litters: Table of litters with their solutions c.
  • b: Fixed sex effects solutions.
  • a: Vector of estimated breeding values for all 15 animals.
  • c: Vector of common environmental (litter) effects for litters of dams 2, 4, and 5.

Chapter 6: Multivariate Models & MACE

Mrode4edExamples.ex_06_1 — Function
ex_06_1() -> NamedTuple

Example 6.1: Multivariate animal model with equal design matrices and no missing records (Mrode & Pocrnic, 2023, Sections 6.1–6.2).

Model Formulation

For two traits measured on the same individuals (weaning gain WWG and post-weaning gain PWG):

\[\begin{bmatrix} y_1 \\ y_2 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} X_1 & 0 \\ 0 & X_2 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} b_1 \\ b_2 \end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix} Z_1 & 0 \\ 0 & Z_2 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} a_1 \\ a_2 \end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix} e_1 \\ e_2 \end{bmatrix}\]

where:

  • Design matrices are identical: $X_1 = X_2 = X$ and $Z_1 = Z_2 = Z$.
  • Genetic (co)variance: $\mathrm{var}(a) = G_0 \otimes A$ with $G_0 = \begin{bmatrix} 20.0 & 18.0 \\ 18.0 & 40.0 \end{bmatrix}$ (genetic correlation $r_g = 18 / \sqrt{20 \times 40} = 0.636$).
  • Residual (co)variance: $\mathrm{var}(e) = R_0 \otimes I$ with $R_0 = \begin{bmatrix} 40.0 & 11.0 \\ 11.0 & 30.0 \end{bmatrix}$ (residual correlation $r_e = 11 / \sqrt{40 \times 30} = 0.318$).

Key Concepts Illustrated

  1. One table for the data: pedigree, sex and both traits of all 8 animals in one DataFrame; all animal-level results (multi-trait and univariate EBV, PEV, reliabilities) are added as columns.
  2. Multi-Trait BLUP: with equal design matrices and no missing records, and traits stacked ($y = \mathrm{vec}(Y)$), the MME are

    \[\begin{bmatrix} R_0^{-1} \otimes X'X & R_0^{-1} \otimes X'Z \\ R_0^{-1} \otimes Z'X & R_0^{-1} \otimes Z'Z + G_0^{-1} \otimes A^{-1} \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix}\hat{b} \\ \hat{a}\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}(R_0^{-1} \otimes X') y \\ (R_0^{-1} \otimes Z') y\end{bmatrix},\]

    written out with kron and compared with the general set-up by mt_stack, mt_blocks, and mt_rinv, which also handles missing records and different design matrices per trait.
  3. Comparison with Univariate BLUP: Demonstrating changes in EBV ranking and information borrowing due to genetic and environmental covariances.
  4. Multi-Trait Reliabilities (Section 6.2.4): Prediction error variance matrix $P = \mathrm{diag}(C^{22})$, with $r_{ij}^2 = 1 - P_{ij} / G_{0, jj}$.
  5. Multi-Trait EBV Partition (Section 6.2.3): Matrix decomposition $\hat{a}_i = W_1 \mathrm{PA}_i + W_2 \mathrm{YD}_i$.

Returns

A NamedTuple with fields:

  • ped: The data with columns ebv_wwg, ebv_pwg, uni_wwg, uni_pwg, pev_wwg, pev_pwg, r2_wwg and r2_pwg added.
  • b: Fixed sex effects for WWG and PWG.
  • a: Matrix of estimated breeding values (rows: 8 animals, cols: WWG and PWG).
  • P: Prediction error variances (PEV) for each animal and trait.

Mrode4edExamples.ex_06_2 — Function
ex_06_2() -> NamedTuple

Example 6.2: Multivariate animal model with equal design matrices and missing records (Mrode & Pocrnic, 2023, Section 6.3).

Model Formulation

When some animals lack records for one or more traits (e.g. calves 4 and 9 have WWG but missing PWG), the residual covariance structure cannot be represented by a simple Kronecker product $R_0 \otimes I$. Instead:

\[R^{-1} = \bigoplus_{i=1}^n R_i^{-1}\]

where $R_i$ is the submatrix of $R_0$ corresponding to the traits observed on individual $i$.

Key Concepts Illustrated

  1. One table for the data: pedigree, sex and both traits of all 9 animals in one DataFrame, with missing for the PWG of calves 4 and 9; multi-trait and univariate EBV are added as columns.
  2. Handling Missing Multi-Trait Records: the MME of Example 6.1 still hold if each missing record is set to 0 and $R^{-1}$ is padded, i.e. a calf with observed traits $S$ contributes $(R_0[S,S])^{-1}$ on those traits and zeros elsewhere:

    \[\begin{bmatrix} (I_2 \otimes X)' R^{-1} (I_2 \otimes X) & (I_2 \otimes X)' R^{-1} (I_2 \otimes Z) \\ (I_2 \otimes Z)' R^{-1} (I_2 \otimes X) & (I_2 \otimes Z)' R^{-1} (I_2 \otimes Z) + G_0^{-1} \otimes A^{-1} \end{bmatrix}.\]

    This form is written out explicitly and compared with mt_stack, mt_blocks, and mt_rinv, which drop the missing records instead and invert the submatrices of $R_0$ once per missing pattern.
  3. Information Borrowing Across Correlated Traits: Even without a record on PWG, calves 4 and 9 obtain PWG EBVs from their own WWG records (via genetic correlation $r_g = 0.636$) and their relatives' records.
  4. Comparison with Univariate Analysis: A univariate analysis of PWG cannot use the WWG records of calves 4 and 9.

Returns

A NamedTuple with fields:

  • ped: The data with columns ebv_wwg, ebv_pwg, uni_wwg and uni_pwg added.
  • b: Fixed sex effect solutions for WWG and PWG.
  • a: Matrix of estimated breeding values for all 9 animals.

Example 6.3: Multivariate animal model with unequal design matrices and multi-trait DYD (Mrode & Pocrnic, 2023, Section 6.4).

Model Formulation

Lactation fat yields in parities 1 and 2 are analyzed as two distinct, correlated traits. Unlike Example 6.1, the fixed effects design matrices differ across traits:

\[\begin{bmatrix} y_1 \\ y_2 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} X_1 & 0 \\ 0 & X_2 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} b_1 \\ b_2 \end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix} Z_1 & 0 \\ 0 & Z_2 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} a_1 \\ a_2 \end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix} e_1 \\ e_2 \end{bmatrix}\]

where:

  • $X_1$ models HYS effects in parity 1, and $X_2$ models HYS effects in parity 2.
  • $G_0 = \begin{bmatrix} 35.0 & 28.0 \\ 28.0 & 30.0 \end{bmatrix}$, $R_0 = \begin{bmatrix} 65.0 & 27.0 \\ 27.0 & 70.0 \end{bmatrix}$.

Key Concepts Illustrated

  1. From repeated records to traits: the lactation records of Example 5.1 (one row per record) are reshaped with unstack to one row per cow, with fat yield and HYS of each parity as columns. Animal-level results are added as columns of the pedigree.
  2. Unequal Fixed Effects Design Matrices: HYS 1–2 occur only in parity 1 and HYS 3–4 only in parity 2, so each trait has its own $X_t$. The MME of Example 6.1 hold with $\mathrm{blockdiag}(X_1, X_2)$ in place of $I_2 \otimes X$:

\[y = Xb + Z u + W m + S pe_{\mathrm{mat}} + e\]

Example 9.1: Animal model with social interaction (indirect genetic / associative) effects (Mrode & Pocrnic, 2023, Chapter 9; Bijma et al., 2007; Muir, 2005).

Model Formulation

When animals are housed in groups (e.g. pigs in pens of three), an individual's phenotype is affected by its own genes (direct breeding value, DBV) and the genes of its pen-mates (social/associative breeding value, SBV):

\[y_i = x_i' b + u_{Di} + \sum_{j \neq i} u_{Sj} + g_k + c_l + e_i^*\]

where:

  • $u_{Di}$ is the direct genetic effect of animal $i$, with variance $\sigma_{AD}^2$.
  • $u_{Sj}$ is the associative genetic effect of pen-mate $j$, with variance $\sigma_{AS}^2$.
  • Direct and associative genetic effects are correlated: $\mathrm{cov}(u_D, u_S) = \sigma_{ADS}$, so $G_0 = \begin{bmatrix} \sigma_{AD}^2 & \sigma_{ADS} \\ \sigma_{ADS} & \sigma_{AS}^2 \end{bmatrix}$.
  • Total Breeding Value (TBV) is the overall effect on the group mean: $\mathrm{TBV}_i = u_{Di} + (n - 1) u_{Si}$ (here $n = 3$).
  • $g_k$ is the random group (pen) environmental effect, with variance $\sigma_g^2 = \rho \sigma_e^2$.
  • $c_l$ is the common litter environmental effect, with variance $\sigma_c^2$.

Key Concepts Illustrated

  1. One table for the data: pedigree, sex, pen, litter and growth rate of all 15 pigs in one DataFrame; DBV, SBV, TBV and the EBV of the model without associative effects are added as columns, and the pen and litter solutions kept in tables of their own.

  3. MME (Eqn 9.6) as $T'T + \Sigma$ with $T = [X\ Z_D\ Z_S\ V\ W]$ and $\Sigma$ holding $\alpha \otimes A^{-1}$ ($\alpha = \sigma_{e^*}^2 G_0^{-1}$) and the variance ratios of the group and litter effects; compared with mme.
  4. Comparison with Standard Animal Model: Animal model ignoring associative effects.
  5. Equivalence with Correlated Residuals Model (Section 9.4): Instead of fitting an explicit random group effect $g$, pen-mates have correlated residuals: $R = \sigma_e^2 [(1 - \rho) I + \rho VV']$, block diagonal $R_i = \sigma_e^2 [(1 - \rho) I_n + \rho J_n]$ per pen, yielding identical fixed and genetic solutions.
  6. Decomposition of DBV and SBV (Section 9.3): Partition into Parent Average (PA) and direct/associative yield deviations (YD).

Example 10.3: Covariance functions and linear spline models (Mrode & Pocrnic, 2023, Sections 10.4 and 10.3.5; Kirkpatrick et al., 1990; Meyer, 2005).

Model Formulation and Theory

Covariance functions provide a continuous representation of genetic (co)variance across age or time. Given a discrete covariance matrix $G$ at ages $t_1, t_2, \dots, t_p$ and normalized Legendre polynomials $\Phi$:

\[G = \Phi C \Phi' \implies \hat{C} = \Phi^{-1} G (\Phi')^{-1}\]

where:

  • $C$ is the coefficient matrix of the covariance function.
  • Covariance between any two ages $t_i$ and $t_j$ is given by $\phi(t_i)' C \phi(t_j)$.
  • Reduced-order approximation (Section 10.4.1): Fitting a $k \times k$ coefficient matrix (here $k = 2$) to an original $3 \times 3$ matrix via Weighted Least Squares (WLS) using the asymptotic covariance matrix of sample (co)variances $V$, evaluated by a $\chi^2$ goodness-of-fit test.
  • Linear spline model (Section 10.3.5): Continuous piecewise linear functions defined by knot points.

Key Concepts Illustrated

  1. Full-Order Fit: Computed directly as $\hat{C} = \Phi^{-1} G (\Phi')^{-1}$ (in Julia Φ \ G / Φ') and verified against covariance_function.
  2. Reduced-Order Fit by WLS: Written out explicitly using the identity $\mathrm{vec}(\Phi C \Phi') = (\Phi \otimes \Phi)\,\mathrm{vec}(C)$. The design matrix for the unique elements is $X = L(\Phi_2 \otimes \Phi_2)D$, where $L$ selects the lower triangle of $G$ and $D$ is the duplication matrix of $C$, verified against covariance_function.
  3. Continuous (Co)variance Trajectory: Evaluates genetic variances and covariances at any age along the trajectory, tabulated over 90–240 days for both the full- and reduced-order covariance functions.

Note on Text Discrepancy

In the linear spline table of Section 10.3.5 (knots at 4, 106, 208, and 310 DIM), the book interchanges the two non-zero weights $(t - t_i)/(t_{i+1} - t_i)$ and $(t_{i+1} - t)/(t_{i+1} - t_i)$ at interior days in milk (assigning the larger weight to the farther knot). Standard linear interpolation is computed here.

Examples 11.9–11.12: Bayesian alphabetic models (BayesA, BayesB, BayesC, and BayesCπ) (Mrode & Pocrnic, 2023, Section 11.7; Meuwissen et al., 2001; Habier et al., 2011).

Model Formulation and Priors

The Bayesian regression model is:

\[y = \mathbf{1}\mu + \sum_{j=1}^m z_j g_j + e\]

with uncentered genotypes $Z$, flat prior on the mean $\mu$, and $e_i \sim N(0, \sigma_e^2)$. The methods differ in the prior distribution placed on marker effects $g_j$ and variances $\sigma_{gj}^2$:

  • BayesA (Example 11.9): Every marker has an effect; marker variances $\sigma_{gj}^2$ are independently drawn from a scaled inverse chi-square distribution: $\sigma_{gj}^2 \sim \chi^{-2}(\nu, S)$. This induces a marginal Student-t prior on $g_j$.
  • BayesB (Example 11.10): Mixture prior with probability $\pi$ that a marker has zero effect ($\sigma_{gj}^2 = 0$) and $1 - \pi$ that $\sigma_{gj}^2 \sim \chi^{-2}(\nu, S)$.
  • BayesC (Example 11.11): Common variance $\sigma_g^2 \sim \chi^{-2}(\nu, S)$ across all active markers, with fixed inclusion probability $1 - \pi$.
  • BayesCπ (Example 11.12): Like BayesC, but the proportion of active markers $1 - \pi$ is also estimated from the data using a uniform, Beta(1, 1), prior on $\pi$.

Key Concepts Illustrated

  1. Fast Gibbs Sampling via Residual Updating: Instead of solving full linear systems each cycle, residuals $e = y - \mathbf{1}\mu - Z g$ are updated one marker at a time:

    \[e_{\mathrm{new}} = e_{\mathrm{old}} + z_j g_{j,\mathrm{old}} - z_j g_{j,\mathrm{new}}\]

    The deterministic starting mean and residuals are checked against the book.
  2. Data and Result Tables: Posterior mean SNP effects from all four Bayesian models are collected alongside the book's printed values in a SNP DataFrame (including marker inclusion probabilities for BayesB and BayesCπ), and the resulting DGVs $M\hat{g}$ are added as columns of the bull table so the methods can be compared across reference bulls and selection candidates.
  3. Behavior of a Monomorphic SNP: SNP 7 is 0 in all reference bulls ($z_7'z_7 = 0$), so the likelihood carries no information about it. Its variance and inclusion indicator are sampled from their priors, giving an inclusion probability of $1 - \pi$ (0.7 in BayesB, $1 - \mathrm{E}(\pi \mid y)$ in BayesCπ) and a posterior mean effect centered on zero.

  1. Initial residual typo: For bull 1 (animal 13), $y_1 = 9.0$, $\hat{\mu} = 9.888$, and marker dosages summing to 9 (6 non-zero SNPs) at $0.05$ give $\hat{e}_1 = 9.0 - 9.888 - 0.45 = -1.338$, whereas the book prints $-1.388$.
  2. Monte Carlo Verification: The book's first-iteration values depend on its specific random numbers, and its posterior means (from 7,000 samples) carry noticeable Monte Carlo error. Here, the deterministic starting step is checked exactly and posterior means from a 50,000-iteration chain are verified within Monte Carlo tolerance.

Example 12.1: Single-step GBLUP (ssGBLUP) (Mrode & Pocrnic, 2023, Chapter 12; Aguilar et al., 2010; Christensen & Lund, 2010).

Model Formulation

Single-step GBLUP integrates genotyped animals and ungenotyped relatives into a single evaluation by replacing the pedigree relationship matrix $A$ with the joint pedigree–genomic relationship matrix $H$:

\[y = Xb + W a + e\]

where $\mathrm{var}(a) = H \sigma_a^2$. The inverse $H^{-1}$ is computed directly without inverting the full $H$ matrix:

\[H^{-1} = A^{-1} + \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 0 \\ 0 & G_t^{-1} - A_{22}^{-1} \end{bmatrix}\]

  • Blending: $G_b = 0.95 G + 0.05 A_{22}$ to guarantee non-singularity.
  • Tuning (Chen et al., 2011): $G_t = a + b G_b$ matches the mean diagonal and off-diagonal elements of $G_b$ to $A_{22}$, ensuring compatibility with the base population of $A$.
  • $hinv(ped, Gt, id)$ builds $H^{-1}$ directly.

Key Concepts Illustrated

  1. Example 12.2: Algorithm for Proven and Young (APY) inverse of $G$ (Mrode & Pocrnic, 2023, Section 12.4; Misztal et al., 2014; Misztal, 2016).

Model Formulation and APY Theory

For large populations, inverting $G$ directly is computationally prohibitive (cubic complexity $O(n^3)$). APY approximates $G^{-1}$ with linear complexity by partitioning genotyped animals into core animals (c) and non-core animals (n):

\[G_{\mathrm{APY}}^{-1} = \begin{bmatrix} G_{cc}^{-1} + G_{cc}^{-1} G_{cn} M_{nn}^{-1} G_{nc} G_{cc}^{-1} & -G_{cc}^{-1} G_{cn} M_{nn}^{-1} \\ -M_{nn}^{-1} G_{nc} G_{cc}^{-1} & M_{nn}^{-1} \end{bmatrix}\]

where $M_{nn}$ is a strictly diagonal matrix with elements:

\[m_{ii} = g_{ii} - g_{ic} G_{cc}^{-1} g_{ci}\]

representing the conditional variance of non-core animal $i$ given the core animals.

Key Concepts Illustrated

  1. Core Animal Selection: Bulls 15, 20, 22, and 26 chosen as core animals.

  4. Comparison of DGVs: Evaluating correlation between DGVs computed using exact $G^{-1}$ versus APY $G_{\mathrm{APY}}^{-1}$ (here $r \approx 0.95$).

Example 15.1: Threshold (probit) sire model for calving ease in three ordered categories (Mrode & Pocrnic, 2023, Sections 15.1–15.2; Gianola & Foulley, 1983; Harville & Mee, 1984).

Model Formulation and Theory

Calving ease is scored in 3 ordered categories (1 = normal, 2 = slight difficulty, 3 = extreme difficulty) governed by an underlying unobservable continuous liability $\ell$:

\[\ell = Xb + Z s + e, \quad e \sim N(0, 1)\]

with fixed thresholds $t_1 < t_2$ (setting $t_0 = -\infty, t_3 = +\infty$) such that an observation falls in category $k$ if $t_{k-1} < \ell \le t_k$. The probability of observing category $k$ given linear predictor $\eta = x' b + z' s$ is:

\[P(Y = k \mid \eta) = \Phi(t_k - \eta) - \Phi(t_{k-1} - \eta)\]

where:

  • $\Phi(\cdot)$ is the standard normal cumulative distribution function (probit link).
  • The residual variance on the liability scale is set to $\sigma_e^2 = 1.0$ for identifiability.
  • Sire transmitting ability variance on the liability scale is $\sigma_s^2 = 1/19$ (variance ratio $\alpha = 19$).
  • Thresholds $t$ and location parameters $\theta = [b; s]$ are estimated jointly by Fisher scoring (iteratively reweighted generalized linear mixed model via threshold_model).
  • Category probabilities by sire subclass are computed with category_probabilities.

Key Concepts Illustrated

  1. Subclass counts vs individual calving records: The data in Table 15.2 are entered as a DataFrame with one row per herd–sex–sire subclass and the counts (n1, n2, n3) in each calving-ease category. For the linear sire model comparison, we expand the subclass counts to one row per calving using stack and row repetition.
  2. Fisher scoring on the liability scale: Thresholds $t = [t_1, t_2]$, fixed herd and sex effects $b$, and sire transmitting abilities $u$ are estimated jointly via threshold_model (with herd 1 and male constrained to zero), and asymptotic standard errors are obtained from the inverse information matrix.
  3. Translating liability solutions back to the probability scale: Because $\hat{u}$ is on the underlying standard-normal liability scale, we compute the predicted probability of each calving-ease category for a given sire as $P_k = \Phi(t_k - \eta) - \Phi(t_{k-1} - \eta)$ (checked against category_probabilities), both for a specific subclass (female calves in herd 1) and averaged across all four herd × sex subclasses. All sire solutions and probabilities are collected in a sires table.

Example 15.2: Joint analysis of a continuous trait (birth weight, BW) and a binary threshold trait (calving difficulty, CD) (Mrode & Pocrnic, 2023, Section 15.3; Foulley et al., 1983).

Let trait 1 be continuous (birth weight, $y$) and trait 2 be binary (calving difficulty, $w \in \{0, 1\}$ governed by an underlying continuous liability $\ell$):

\[\begin{aligned} y &= X_1 b_1 + Z_1 u_1 + e_1 \\ \ell &= X_2 b_2 + Z_2 u_2 + e_2 \end{aligned}\]

with sire transmitting abilities $u = [u_1';\ u_2']' \sim N(0, G_0 \otimes A)$ and residuals $e \sim N(0, R_0 \otimes I)$, where:

\[G_0 = \begin{bmatrix} \sigma_{u_1}^2 & \sigma_{u_{12}} \\ \sigma_{u_{12}} & \sigma_{u_2}^2 \end{bmatrix}, \quad R_0 = \begin{bmatrix} \sigma_{e_1}^2 & \sigma_{e_{12}} \\ \sigma_{e_{12}} & 1 \end{bmatrix}\]

(the residual variance of liability is fixed to 1 for identifiability).

\[\ell \mid y = X_2 b_2 + Z_2 u_2 + \beta (y - X_1 b_1 - Z_1 u_1) + e_{2\mid 1} = X_2 \tau + Z_2 \nu + \beta (y - \bar{y}) + e_{2\mid 1}\]

where:

  • $\beta = \sigma_{e_{12}} / \sigma_{e_1}^2$ is the residual regression of liability on BW.
  • $\sigma_{e_{2\mid 1}}^2 = 1 - \beta^2 \sigma_{e_1}^2$ is the conditional residual variance of liability.
  • $\tau$ and $\nu = u_2 - \beta u_1$ are the reparameterized fixed and sire effects on conditional liability, with BW centred on its mean $\bar{y} = 43.02$.

For a specific subclass (origin 2, season 1, male calf) and sire $j$, the probability of a difficult calving is $\Phi\!\left(\hat{\tau}_{\text{sub}} + \hat{\nu}_j + \hat{\beta}(\hat{b}_{1,\text{sub}} - \bar{y})\right)$.

Sections 17.3 and 17.5: Variance components of a sire model by Henderson's Method 3 and by fitting canonical sums of squares (Mrode & Pocrnic, 2023, Sections 17.3 and 17.5; Henderson, 1953).

Model Formulation

For the sire model $y = Xb + Zs + e$ with $\mathrm{var}(s) = I \sigma_s^2$ and $\mathrm{var}(e) = I \sigma_e^2$:

  1. Henderson's Method 3 (Section 17.3) equates reductions in sums of squares to their expectations:
    • Fixed-effects reduction: $R(b) = y' X (X'X)^- X' y = F$.
    • Full-model reduction: $R(b, s) = \hat{b}' X' y + \hat{s}' Z' y = F + S$.
    • Sire sum of squares adjusted for fixed effects: $S = R(b, s) - R(b)$.
    • Residual sum of squares: $R = y'y - R(b, s)$.
    With $S_X = I - X (X'X)^- X'$, the expectations are $\mathrm{E}(R) = (N - r(X, Z)) \sigma_e^2$ and $\mathrm{E}(S) = \mathrm{tr}(Z' S_X Z) \sigma_s^2 + (r(X, Z) - r(X)) \sigma_e^2$, giving unbiased estimators:

    \[\hat{\sigma}_e^2 = \frac{R}{N - r(X, Z)}, \quad \hat{\sigma}_s^2 = \frac{S - (r(X, Z) - r(X)) \hat{\sigma}_e^2}{\mathrm{tr}(Z' S_X Z)}.\]

  2. Canonical Sums of Squares (Section 17.5) Diagonalizing $Z' S_X Z$ forms orthogonal sire contrasts $Q's$ with eigenvalues $w_i$ and independent mean squares $\mathrm{E}(\mathrm{MS}_i) = \sigma_e^2 + w_i \sigma_s^2$, along with $\mathrm{E}(\mathrm{MS}_e) = \sigma_e^2$. Writing $\mathrm{MS} = C\theta + \epsilon$ with $\theta = [\sigma_e^2;\ \sigma_s^2]$:
    • OLS: $\hat{\theta} = (C'C)^{-1} C' \mathrm{MS}$.
    • Iterated GLS: $\hat{\theta} = (C' V^{-1} C)^{-1} C' V^{-1} \mathrm{MS}$ with $V = \mathrm{diag}\!\left(2\,\mathrm{E}(\mathrm{MS}_i)^2 / \nu_i\right)$ updated at each iterate.

Key Concepts Illustrated

  1. Method 3 written out from projections: We compute $R(b)$ and $R(b, s)$ directly using pinv, evaluate $\mathrm{tr}(Z' S_X Z)$, and verify the reductions and variance estimates against henderson3.
  2. OLS and iterated GLS written out: From the canonical sire contrasts (sire_contrasts), OLS is simply C \ ms, and iterated GLS is a 3-line loop updating the diagonal weight matrix $V^{-1}$; both are checked against fit_mean_squares, and the estimates of all three methods are compared in a table est.

Notes on Text Discrepancies

In Section 17.5, the book prints 0.143 and 0.079 for the unweighted OLS fit (exact OLS on the three mean squares gives 0.151 and 0.062), and prints 0.216 and 0.234 for the "estimated variances" (here the asymptotic sampling variances are 0.051 and 0.062, with standard errors 0.226 and 0.250). The iterated GLS estimates (0.163, 0.047) match the book.

Example 19.5: Reduced Animal Model (RAM) with maternal effects solved by Gauss–Seidel iteration (Mrode & Pocrnic, 2023, Section 19.5.3).

Model Formulation

For the maternal RAM of ex_08_2:

\[y = Xb + Z_1 a_p + Z_2 m_p + S pe + e^*\]

where $a_p$ and $m_p$ are the direct and maternal breeding values of parents only (animals 1–9). Absorbing the direct Mendelian sampling of non-parent calves (animals 10–14) into $e^*$ gives heterogeneous residual variances:

  • For records of parent calves (animals 5–9): $\sigma_{e^*}^2 = \sigma_e^2 = 350$ ($R_{ii}^{-1} = 1/350 \approx 0.002857$).
  • For records of non-parent calves (animals 10–14): $\sigma_{e^*}^2 = \sigma_e^2 + \frac{1}{2} g_{11} = 350 + 75 = 425$ ($R_{ii}^{-1} = 1/425 \approx 0.002353$).

Key Concepts Illustrated

  1. Gauss–Seidel on an unconstrained singular system: Because the fixed effects (3 herds and 2 pens) are linearly dependent, the MME coefficient matrix is singular. Without imposing a constraint, Gauss–Seidel still converges to a valid solution of the normal equations (which specific generalized-inverse solution it reaches depends on the starting vector).
  2. Invariance of estimable functions and BLUPs: Comparing the unconstrained Gauss–Seidel solution with the constrained solution of ex_08_2 ($\mathrm{herd}_1 = 0$) shows that while the individual herd and pen numbers differ, every estimable cell mean $\mathrm{herd}_i + \mathrm{pen}_j$ and all random genetic and permanent environmental solutions (direct, maternal, pe) are identical.

Note on Text Discrepancy

The dataset is that of Table 8.1 (ex_08_1 and ex_08_2), where the second fixed factor is called "Pen" (levels 1 and 2); in Example 19.5 the book labels those same two levels "Sex" (Male and Female). In addition, because Mrode's relative stopping criterion divides by $\|x^{(k)}\|^2$ (which is dominated by the herd means near 30), stopping at $10^{-14}$ leaves errors of order $10^{-5}$ in the random effects; we iterate to $10^{-24}$ so that the random effects and cell means match ex_08_2 to 8 decimal places.
